# Brief — "How to Honestly Talk About the Future of AI: A Three-Layer Framework"

slug: honest-ai-futures · status: ✍️ drafting · language: English first, Chinese version after

## Why this is the first article
It establishes the author's brand (calibrated honesty), introduces the project's signature
method (🟢/🟡/🔴), and uses the SO null result as a lived, credible example. One piece sets up
the whole body of work.

## The one takeaway
*Most AI-future talk fails not because the predictions are wrong, but because it blurs three
different kinds of claim. Separate them — what we know, what we can infer, what we must choose
— and you can think (and argue) about the future honestly, without either false certainty or
useless agnosticism.*

## Claim-type spine
🟡 analogical/methodological at the core (a framework is a 🔴-ish design for *how to reason*),
demonstrated with one 🟢 empirical anchor (the SO study) and applied to 🟡/🔴 examples.

## Structure (target ~1,800–2,200 words)
1. **Hook (real story).** We ran an honest study on Stack Overflow after ChatGPT; we
   pre-registered, and the data killed our hypothesis — twice. The discomfort of that, and
   what it taught: the value wasn't the finding, it was knowing *which kind of claim* we were
   allowed to make at each step.
2. **The problem.** AI-future discourse is drowning in confident nonsense — not because people
   are dumb, but because three very different kinds of statement get worn as if they were one.
3. **The three layers**, each with an AI-future example and its honesty standard:
   - 🟢 Empirical — what we know (data; falsifiable). *e.g.* UBI experiments' wellbeing results.
   - 🟡 Analogical — what we can infer (history + mechanism, with breakpoints stated). *e.g.*
     the Industrial-Revolution comparison.
   - 🔴 Normative/design — what we must choose (values; robustness across futures). *e.g.* what
     to prepare for a post-labour society.
4. **The cardinal sin:** dressing a 🔴 or 🟡 as a 🟢. Examples from real discourse.
5. **Why this is freeing, not paralysing.** You can be bold about big questions *and* honest,
   if each sentence wears its true badge. Tetlock/superforecasting + robust decision-making as
   precedent: serious people work on the unprovable without faking certainty.
6. **Close.** This is the rule I'm holding myself to in everything I write here. Invitation to
   hold me to it. (Sets up the project.)
