## Watchlist movement

- **New to log (outside window, June 20):** Siddiq & Zhang, "Human Capital, AI, and Labor
  Commoditization" (UCLA Anderson; arXiv:2606.21880, submitted 20 Jun 2026).
  🟢 with caveats (economics working paper; DiD design around ChatGPT release; real market
  data from Upwork; **preprint, not peer-reviewed**; Upwork is online freelance labour, not
  the full economy — extrapolation to salaried employment requires care).
  *What it actually shows:* Using high-dimensional text embeddings of worker profiles plus a
  DiD design, the study finds that in more AI-exposed job categories on Upwork, (a) the
  predictive importance of human capital (skills, reputation) for labour demand **declines**
  and (b) the importance of **price rises**; demand reallocates toward lower-priced workers
  and the premium previously earned by workers with strong human capital erodes. Authors
  frame this as a **"commoditization" effect**: AI levels the playing field downward — the
  distinctiveness of skilled freelancers is worth less, cheaper alternatives become more
  competitive.
  *Limits to state honestly:* Upwork is a gig/freelance market, not a representative labour
  sample; the ChatGPT shock is not the only thing that changed; exposure measure is
  category-based, not individual-use; preprint. The online-freelance channel may be an
  *early and extreme* signal (no employment contracts, pure price competition) that does not
  yet generalise to salaried employment. The commoditization framing is the authors'
  interpretation, not a direct measure.
  *Why it matters for the meaning project:* This is the **SDT-competence threat** made
  concrete in market data. SDT holds that the *sense of distinctiveness and mastery* — being
  valued for one's craft, not just one's price — is a core psychological need (competence)
  that work can satisfy. If AI erodes the premium on skilled human capital and turns
  knowledge work into a price competition, it threatens that competence satisfaction from the
  demand side, not only from the task-substitution side. Pairs with the PwC "raised floor"
  finding (entry-level skills bar raised out of reach) and Lee et al.'s (2026) finding that
  passive AI use corrodes self-efficacy — the commoditization mechanism could make passive
  use more likely (compete on price → depend on AI output). Not yet an evidence-base
  candidate (preprint, online-freelance-specific, caveats above); flag for peer-review
  tracking.
  → idea: "commoditized competence" — if AI turns cognitive work into a price war, the
  competence-satisfaction route to meaning is blocked from both ends: skills matter less to
  the market AND AI can do the tasks that built the sense of mastery.
