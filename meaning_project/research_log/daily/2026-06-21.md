## Watchlist movement

- **New to log (outside window, May 6):** ILO, "The Aggregation Paradox of AI: Why do
  micro-economic productivity gains from AI disappear at scale" (ILO Research, 6 May 2026;
  https://www.ilo.org/publications/aggregation-paradox-ai-why-do-micro-economic-productivity-gains-ai).
  🟡 (ILO institutional analysis; full primary text not retrieved directly — reconstructed
  from search summaries; treat as indicative until verified from primary text).
  *What it actually shows:* Current evidence places AI in the early phase of a **J-curve**:
  micro-level experiments show large task-specific productivity gains, but diffusion and
  organisational redesign remain limited. Historical precedent from electrification and ICT:
  aggregate productivity only rose after substantial organisational change and institutional
  adaptation (measured in decades) — AI is likely to follow a similar path, though with
  broader reach into cognitive and service-sector tasks. Productivity gains are also hard to
  measure: they are embedded in intangibles and in quality/variety/speed improvements that
  standard output metrics do not capture. *Policy caveat stated explicitly in the report:*
  without targeted intervention on skills, digital infrastructure, social protection,
  competition, and collective bargaining, AI risks *widening* productivity and income gaps
  across firms, workers, and countries rather than closing them.
  *Relevance for the meaning project:* The J-curve and aggregation-paradox framing directly
  addresses the MANIFESTO's **"speed vs adjustment"** analogy breakpoint. If aggregate
  productivity gains are invisible in the near term (despite real micro effects), the political
  case for distributing AI gains — and for funding the meaning-sustaining institutions that
  the project's 🔴 policy proposals depend on — is weakened by the measurement gap itself.
  The electrification/ICT comparison also tightens the "historical precedent" analogy: past
  technology revolutions did eventually raise aggregate productivity, but the lag was decades.
  This supports the project's existing framing: the core challenge is not "will AI ever pay
  off?" but "what do people do for meaning *in the lag?*" Pairs with and contextualises the
  ILO June 1 GenAI review (which also reports no clear macro-level signal from AI on output
  or employment). Together, the two papers sketch an ILO-framed picture: real micro effects,
  invisible macro effects, organisational and institutional adaptation still pending.
  **Flag for retrieval:** worth a proper evidence-base entry if primary text is accessible —
  the J-curve / aggregation-paradox framing names a mechanism the project doesn't yet have a
  dedicated anchor for.
