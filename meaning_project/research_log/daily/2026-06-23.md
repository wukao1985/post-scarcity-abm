## Items

- **"AI and the US labour market: effects on employment growth"** — ECB Economic Bulletin
  4/2026 Focus, European Central Bank; primary:
  https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/economic-bulletin/focus/2026/html/ecb.ebbox202604_01~d9259db536.en.html
  Coverage: Invezz (Jun 22), Yahoo Finance, Global Banking & Finance, allwork.space.
  🟢 with caveats (ECB institutional economists; difference-in-difference with industry/COVID
  controls; but exposure measure is capability-based — "substitution risk" — not deployment/
  usage data; the 2019-2025 window includes a long pre-ChatGPT period; specifics corroborated
  across multiple secondary sources, primary retrieved via search).
  *What it actually shows:* Using a DiD design on US occupational employment data (2019–2025),
  the ECB finds **employment has already begun to reallocate** along the AI-substitution-risk
  gradient: high-risk occupations (e.g. economists, graphic designers) declined ~4% on average;
  low-risk occupations (e.g. electricians, high school teachers) grew ~13%; the gap across risk
  groups is ~15pp. The share of high-risk jobs in total US employment fell from 35% to 33%;
  low-risk jobs rose from 23% to 25%. **Crucially: no significant wage impact across the same
  period.** Junior staff in highly exposed sectors flagged as particularly vulnerable. The
  aggregate conclusion: "the overall effect on aggregate employment or wages in the US has been
  muted so far."
  *Limits:* capability-based exposure (what AI *could* substitute) rather than measured
  deployment; the long window includes 2019–2022 (pre-GenAI), so the reallocation signal is not
  cleanly GenAI-caused; "junior staff vulnerable" from secondary summaries, not primary text;
  ECB economists studying the US from outside — no unique data access.
  *Why it matters for the meaning project:* This joins the converging institutional picture
  (Audoly/NY Fed, Massenkoff/Anthropic, Yale Budget Lab, Goldman Sachs, BLS sector trends)
  but adds something: **the clearest DiD evidence so far that employment reallocation is
  already underway**, even as wages and aggregate employment remain unaffected. The specific
  occupations named — economists, graphic designers — are squarely in the cognitive/creative
  professional tier, not the routine clerical tier of earlier automation fears. The wage-null
  combined with employment reallocation is a meaningful combination for the project: displaced
  workers are not finding equivalent wages elsewhere (yet?). The junior-staff vulnerability
  note is consistent with the Stanford/ADP Canaries finding (though that finding is contested by
  the NY Fed remote-work confound). Does not change the existing synthesis — it reinforces the
  "early reallocation without aggregate wage harm, but the adjustment loop is not yet closing
  for the displaced" reading. Not a candidate for the evidence_base yet (needs primary-text
  methodological review to assess the exposure measure quality); flag for review if it passes
  peer review.

## Watchlist movement

- **Employment reallocation signal:** The ECB DiD result is the clearest occupational-mix shift
  documented by a major independent institution so far — 15pp gap between high-risk and
  low-risk occupation employment growth 2019-2025. The share shift (high-risk 35%→33%, low-risk
  23%→25%) is small but consistent with the direction of other signals. The "new jobs
  mechanism" (low-risk jobs growing) is visible — but it's growing in occupations structurally
  different from the declining ones, so the question of whether displaced cognitive/creative
  workers are landing in the low-risk category (electricians, teachers) is unanswered.
- **Wage-null holding:** ECB wage-null consistent with IMF SDN/2026/001 finding that AI-specific
  skill postings carry higher wages but no aggregate employment gain. Together: some wage premium
  at the top of the skill distribution, no wage harm at the aggregate — but the aggregate may
  be masking deterioration for the displaced sub-population.
- **No movement** on the contested attribution picture (NY Fed remote-work confound, contract-
  buffer null, Massenkoff hiring-flow narrowing, Audoly postings-null vs employment reallocation
  now observed by ECB). Still to watch: BLS occupational employment update; primary-text
  retrieval for ILO June 1 GenAI review and ILO Aggregation Paradox paper.
