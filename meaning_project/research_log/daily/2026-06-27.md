## Watchlist movement

- **New to log (outside window — May 2025):** Humlum & Vestergaard, *"Still Waters, Rapid
  Currents: Early Labor Market Transformation under Generative AI"* (NBER WP 33777; SSRN
  5250742; Chicago Booth CAAI). 🟢 with caveats (Danish administrative records + large-scale
  adoption surveys; DiD design; strong identification; **single-country** — Denmark's strong
  worker protections, sector mix, and co-determination may buffer employment effects relative
  to the US; **May 2025** — now a year old, so contemporaneous with the other attribution
  studies in the cluster, not a new signal).
  *What it actually shows:* Links employer-level AI chatbot adoption surveys (most employers in
  AI-exposed occupations had adopted chatbot initiatives by the survey window) to Danish
  administrative earnings and hours records. DiD finds **precise null effects on earnings and
  recorded hours** at both worker and workplace levels — ruling out effects larger than ~2%
  two years post-ChatGPT. But the surface calm conceals structural reorganisation: **new AI
  tasks** (content generation, AI oversight, AI integration) proliferate substantially; adopters
  transition into higher-paying occupations where AI chatbots are more relevant — though "still
  too few to move average earnings."
  *Limits to state honestly:* Denmark only; Danish co-determination slows restructuring and
  adds a legal buffer absent in the US/UK; one-year window post-ChatGPT may be too short for
  full adjustment; administrative data does not capture quality-of-work or meaning dimensions.
  The "rapid currents" (task reorganisation) finding is survey-based, not from the admin records.
  *Why it matters for the meaning project:* Adds high-quality causal identification to the
  existing macro-null / micro-reorganisation cluster (ILO J-curve pair, IMF wages-without-
  headcount, Audoly postings-null). The **"still waters / rapid currents" framing is useful
  analytically**: aggregate calm is real AND task structure is shifting underneath — which is
  precisely the scenario the "joy of work" analysis (Ranjit et al.) warns about. Aggregate
  employment stability does NOT mean psychological stability, because the *tasks* reorganised
  away may be the ones that produced competence and autonomy satisfaction. Pairs well with Lee
  et al. (passive AI use → self-efficacy erosion) and ILO WP170 (algorithmic management →
  autonomy erosion): the "rapid currents" of task reorganisation are the mechanism by which
  meaning can be eroded even in a world of stable headline employment. Pending decision:
  whether to add to evidence_base as a 🟢 anchor for the "null aggregate / structural
  reorganisation" sub-claim — would need to note the Denmark-specificity.
