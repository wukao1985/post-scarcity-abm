## Items

- **BLS Employment Situation — June 2026** — U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (official; released
  today, 2 Jul 2026; https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm). 🟢 (primary government
  data). *Shows:* nonfarm payrolls +57,000 (in line with the ~+36,000 12-month average), unemployment rate flat
  at 4.2%; April and May payrolls revised
  down a combined 74,000 (April 179K→148K, May 172K→129K); leisure/hospitality −61,000;
  **information and financial-activities sectors — the two highest-AI-exposure sectors this
  project has been tracking (prior 12-month readings: financial −107K, information −342K since
  Nov-22) — showed "little or no change" this specific month.** *Limits:* one month of data;
  "little or no change" from BLS could mean the earlier decline is stabilizing, or just be
  monthly noise inside a still-declining 12-month trend — this report alone can't distinguish
  the two, and BLS assigns no cause either way. *Why it matters:* this resolves the "BLS June
  data — watch information/financial for acceleration" flag carried since 2026-06-27. The
  answer is **no acceleration this month** — mild reinforcement of the macro-calm reading
  (Humlum & Vestergaard, Audoly et al., ECB, Massenkoff & McCrory), not a new signal either way.
  Downward revisions to April/May are a general labour-market softening note, not AI-specific.

- **California AI-Unemployment Tracker (CAIT)** — California Employment Development Dept +
  California Policy Lab (UC); launched 25 Jun 2026, findings summarized in a wave of coverage
  including a 2 Jul 2026 Insurance Journal piece; https://capolicylab.org/california-ai-unemployment-tracker/.
  🟡/🟢 (first *government-partnered, ongoing* AI-attributed unemployment-claims dashboard —
  a new institutional data source, methodologically closer to Massenkoff & McCrory's
  observed-Claude-usage exposure measure than to capability-based indices; but UI claims data:
  self-reported occupations, excludes non-filers/ineligible/labour-force-exiters, "capability
  exposure ≠ causation" per the tracker's own stated limitations, and it is *administrative
  claims* not a peer-reviewed causal study). *Shows (through May 2026):* **no statewide surge**
  in AI-related claims since ChatGPT's 2022 release — but claims from **college-educated
  workers in high-AI-exposure occupations rose and stayed elevated** since 2022, and the **San
  Francisco Bay Area + information/professional-services sectors show a sharp, sustained
  increase**. *Why it matters:* a new, ongoing, government-backed data source for the
  contested AI-labour attribution cluster — its "no aggregate surge, but concentrated in
  high-exposure/high-education/tech-hub subgroups" finding sits comfortably inside the existing
  synthesis (aggregate calm + selective, structural displacement) rather than overturning it,
  but it's a new *instrument* worth tracking monthly since — unlike one-off papers — it updates
  continuously. *Not yet an evidence_base candidate* (too new, preliminary, single-state); flag
  for monthly tracking as an ongoing early-warning indicator.
