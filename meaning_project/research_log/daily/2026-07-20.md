## Items

- **Lee, "The Dual Face of Workplace AI: A Typology of Digital Work Configurations and Job
  Quality Implications in Europe"** — *New Technology, Work and Employment* (Wiley,
  DOI: 10.1111/ntwe.70040). 🟡 (**primary text not retrieved** — Wiley paywalled the article,
  direct fetch returned 403; reconstructed from search-engine abstract snippets only, so treat as
  provisional pending a primary read; **publication date could not be confirmed** — flagging this
  as a possible catch-up item outside the strict 24–48h window, in the same spirit as prior misses
  logged on 2026-07-14/15/17, rather than silently dropping it). Uses the European Commission
  Joint Research Centre's **AIM-WORK survey** (2024–2025 fielding; N=50,462 workers, 27 EU
  countries) — the same official dataset behind an already-known JRC report (Mariscal de Gante,
  Gonzalez Vazquez & Fernandez Macias, 25 Feb 2026, on "platformisation," itself 5 months stale and
  not logged as new today). *Shows (as reported by secondary sources — unverified against primary):*
  a four-way typology by AI-tool-usage intensity × algorithmic-control exposure — **Non-Digital
  Baseline, Heavily Controlled, Digitally Empowered, Managed Augmenter**. "Digitally Empowered"
  workers earn a **21-percentage-point wage premium** over their country's median with preserved
  autonomy, but are only **3.7% of European employment** (no country above 5.5%). "Heavily
  Controlled" workers show reduced autonomy and work intensification **with no compensating wage
  premium**. Workers with statistically similar AI-tool-usage intensity land in very different
  outcomes depending on the surrounding governance/control structure (Digitally Empowered vs
  Managed Augmenter) — i.e. it isn't AI exposure alone that predicts job quality, it's whether the
  tool comes bundled with algorithmic control. *Tension flagged:* the companion JRC report using
  the *same* AIM-WORK survey (Feb 2026) found platformisation's negative job-quality associations
  "almost exclusively concentrated in Central-Eastern EU Member States, with Western EU generally
  neutral" — a more geographically hedged picture than the "consistently negative...regardless of
  AI tool access" framing attributed to Lee's paper in search summaries. Unresolved without a
  primary read of both. *Why it matters:* this is the first **large-scale, quantitative,
  official-survey-based** empirical test this project has found of the ILO WP170 autonomy-erosion
  thread, which has sat at 🟡 ("primary text not retrieved... treat as indicative") since 30 Apr
  2026. If the numbers hold up on a primary read, it gives WP170's qualitative claim a hard
  anchor: only a **thin sliver (3.7%) of European AI-touching workers get the autonomy-preserving,
  well-paid configuration**; the wage premium for AI use is conditional on organisational control
  structure, not automatic. → **candidate for evidence_base, contingent on a primary-text
  read-through** (both Lee's paper and the JRC platformisation report) to confirm the numbers and
  resolve the geographic-scope tension before treating as anything stronger than 🟡.
  https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ntwe.70040
