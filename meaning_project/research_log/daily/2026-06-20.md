## Watchlist movement

- **New to log (borderline timing, June 17):** Tandfonline meta-analysis — "Meta-analysis of
  employee well-being and perception towards robot, artificial intelligence, and service
  automation" (DOI: 10.1080/23311908.2026.2672185; 47 studies, pre-registered). *What it
  actually shows:* significant correlation between employee *perceptions of* AI/robotics/
  automation and well-being, but **effect size is low-to-moderate and heterogeneity is high**.
  Honesty limits: "perceptions of" rather than actual use outcomes; causal direction ambiguous
  (does negative AI attitude cause lower well-being, or vice versa?); published in Cogent
  Business & Management (open-access Taylor & Francis series, not top-tier). *Relevance:*
  broadly confirmatory of the existing synthesis — the Giuntella reconciliation anchor already
  captures the population-average null with actual-user signals; the high heterogeneity is
  consistent with Lee et al.'s active/passive moderation. Adds a pooled effect-size number
  (low-to-moderate) but doesn't move the picture. Track only if an independent replication
  or moderator analysis clarifies the heterogeneity.

- **New to log (outside window, June 1):** ILO "The Impact of GenAI on Jobs, Productivity
  and Work Organization: A Review of the Empirical Evidence" (June 1, 2026;
  https://www.ilo.org/publications/impact-genai-jobs-productivity-and-work-organization-review-empirical).
  Drawing on experiments, firm-level data, platform studies and new surveys across 7 countries
  (Australia, Denmark, Germany, Korea, Kuwait, UK, US). *Key claims from secondary coverage:*
  productivity gains are real but often unverified and uneven; most firms report little
  measurable employment impact so far; productivity gains concentrated in larger, digitally
  advanced enterprises. Needs primary text retrieval to verify specifics. Would slot near the
  existing ILO WP170 and ILO–World Bank entries in the evidence base. **Flag for retrieval:**
  if the primary text is accessible, this is worth a proper evidence-base entry given its
  multi-country evidence base and ILO institutional standing.

- **Bousquet (JBE 2026) — date uncertain:** "Lonely Work: Gig Work, Workplace Technology
  and Working-Class Loneliness" (J. Business Ethics, 2026; Springer link s10551-026-06289-6).
  Philosophical/normative argument (🔴) that platform gig work, automation tools, and
  monitoring technology have desocialized work — stripping away Jahoda's latent social
  functions — and that this desocialization is a matter of justice warranting regulatory
  response. If confirmed in the window, would be a useful 🔴 companion to ILO WP170 (autonomy/
  surveillance) and the Aalto/Folk & Dunn loneliness findings — explicitly naming the structural
  desocialization of low-income work as a justice issue, not merely a wellbeing risk.
