## Items

- **Goldman Sachs U.S. Daily: AI Adoption Tracker** — Elsie Peng, Goldman Sachs (April 2026
  note; updated coverage June 1, 2026 via Fortune/Yahoo Finance). Proprietary — read through
  journalism; primary GS report not publicly available.
  🟢 (payroll-data-based, peer-methodology — with honest caveat: proprietary Goldman model,
  accessed via secondary coverage; not independently verifiable from primary).
  *What it actually shows:* GS economists separated AI's two competing payroll effects using
  standard AI exposure scores combined with an IMF complementarity index. AI **substitution**
  reduced US payroll growth by ~25,000 gross jobs/month over the past year; AI **augmentation**
  added back ~9,000 jobs/month — net ~16,000 jobs/month lost (April estimate). By early June
  the net was revised down to ~11,000/month, with the improvement attributed to data-centre
  construction adding ~9,000 positions (not to a reversal in substitution). Unemployment
  impact: substitution raised rate by ~0.16pp, augmentation lowered by ~0.06pp, net +0.10pp.
  Most-substituted roles: telephone operators, insurance claims clerks, bill collectors.
  Gen Z concentration is in exactly these routine white-collar admin roles. *Limits (state
  honestly):* proprietary model, read through journalism; methodology combines GS-internal
  and IMF exposure scores — cannot assess identification fully; the NY Fed (logged 2026-05-31)
  found job-posting declines predated ChatGPT and tracked the rate cycle, so macro confounds
  remain a live concern even for payroll-level analysis. *Why it matters:* this is the most
  concrete attempt yet to separate substitution from augmentation in actual payroll data rather
  than theoretical exposure scores. The gross substitution figure (~25K/month) is significant;
  the net figure (~11–16K/month) is modest but non-zero. The fact that the apparent
  improvement is from construction — not reduced substitution — is important: the "new jobs"
  mechanism is currently physical infrastructure, not the re-employment of displaced admin
  workers. Pairs with the Stanford/ADP young-cohort finding and IMF SDN/2026/001 already in
  evidence_base.
  → watchlist: the gross/net split will widen or narrow as the data-centre buildout plateaus —
  the next Goldman update will signal whether substitution is accelerating faster than
  augmentation can offset.

- **"Artificial intelligence and the wellbeing of workers"** — Giuntella, Koenig & Stella;
  *Scientific Reports* 15(1): 20087 (June 23, 2025). PMC12185714.
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-98241-3
  🟢 (longitudinal survey, difference-in-differences design; not pre-registered as far as
  known; observational — confounding possible).
  *What it actually shows:* Two decades of German Socio-Economic Panel (SOEP) data; constructs
  individual AI exposure from first-employment occupation; event-study and DiD comparison of
  AI-exposed vs non-exposed workers. Main result: **no evidence AI occupational exposure harmed
  workers' mental health or subjective wellbeing on average**; small improvements in health
  status and health satisfaction, attributed to reduced physical job intensity. *Critical
  nuance:* when restricting to workers who **self-report actually using AI tools** in the
  workplace, there are "indications of declining life and job satisfaction." *Limits (state
  honestly):* SOEP data ends around 2020 — this captures early AI adoption, not the generative-
  AI era post-2022; occupational exposure is an average, not a direct measure of individual
  AI interaction; the self-report subsample is smaller and subject to selection; German labour
  market (strong co-determination, works councils) may buffer harms that US/gig workers face.
  *Why it matters:* This is the primary reconciliation anchor for what had been a gap in the
  project's evidence: the aggregate population-level result (no harm on average) is consistent
  with the task-level experimental result (Lee et al., 2026 — passive AI use harms self-
  efficacy/meaning) because they operate at different levels. The Giuntella finding captures
  the average across all uses (much of which is still incidental/peripheral AI exposure); Lee
  et al. isolates the mechanism for heavy passive users. The self-report nuance in Giuntella
  (declining life/job satisfaction for actual AI tool users) bridges them: it suggests that
  as AI use penetrates more deeply into actual daily work, the null average may not hold. The
  project should cite both and be explicit about the levels of analysis. Note: Germany's
  institutional context makes direct US generalisation uncertain.
  → candidate for evidence_base (average vs. actual-use nuance; reconciles aggregate null
  with Lee et al. mechanism).
  → idea: "the average lies" — population-level wellbeing studies may mask the specific harm
  pathway; the risk concentrates in heavy, passive users and will grow as AI is embedded
  into more workflows.

## Watchlist movement

- **Substitution vs augmentation (analogy breakpoint #1):** The Goldman Tracker adds the
  first payroll-based gross/net decomposition: ~25K substitution destroyed vs ~9K augmentation
  created = ~16K net lost (now ~11K with construction buffer). The gross substitution figure
  is much larger than the net — so augmentation is real but not yet keeping pace with
  displacement. The construction buffer is a physical-infrastructure effect, not a re-
  employment-of-displaced-workers effect: does not support "new jobs" mechanism for the
  exposed workers. Monitor as construction plateaus in 2027.
- **Speed vs adjustment (#2):** No new movement today; the Goldman figure is consistent with
  the "early innings, not yet macro-scale" read from prior logs. 0.10pp unemployment increase
  is real but not yet the 1–2pp disruption thresholds from prior analogy breakpoints.
- **The "new jobs" mechanism (#3):** Goldman data reinforces the IMF SDN/2026/001 finding —
  AI-enabled growth is creating *some* new jobs (data-centre construction, augmentation-type
  roles), but the jobs being created are not the ones being lost (admin/routine white-collar
  vs. physical infrastructure). The mechanism is not yet closing the loop for displaced workers.
