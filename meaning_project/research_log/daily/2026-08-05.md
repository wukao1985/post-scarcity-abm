## Items

- **Indeed Hiring Lab — Q2 2026 Labor Market Outlook Survey** — [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://www.hiringlab.org/2026/08/05/q2-labor-market-outlook-survey/), published today. 🟡 (inaugural quarterly survey of ~120 economists/labour experts, fielded by Pulsenomics in partnership with Indeed, July 2026 — a **forecast/expectations panel**, not a measured outcome; single wave, no track record yet, so weight accordingly). One sentence on what it actually shows: a narrow majority (52%) of surveyed economists expect AI to be "at least a mild drag on employment" over the next year (35% expect net gains, 13% no effect), expect AI to *reshuffle* rather than eliminate white-collar roles (Admin Support/Marketing/HR as likely losers, Data & Analytics/IT Infrastructure as likely gainers, Software Development split), and 57% expect downward wage pressure on *college-educated* workers vs 34% for non-degree holders — alongside a modest cooling forecast (job-postings index −1.4% through mid-2027, unemployment drifting to ~4.4%). Why it matters: it's a fresh, dated data point (not a recirculating item) that pairs with the existing "expectations vs. outcomes" watchlist thread (Yotzov's employer/worker forecast gap, Massenkoff & McCrory's flow signal) — another instance of professional forecasters leaning mildly negative and specifically flagging *wage* pressure on the educated/white-collar tier, which is the same population this project's meaning question centres on. Not a candidate for evidence_base as a standalone pillar (it is opinion/forecast data, like Yotzov, not realised outcomes) — folds into the existing expectations-gap thread instead.
