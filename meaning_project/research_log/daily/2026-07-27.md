## Items

- **"Trapped Workers: A Network Analysis of Worker Mobility in the AI Economy"** — Bipartisan
  Policy Center, issue brief, 23 Jul 2026 (verified from primary:
  https://bipartisanpolicy.org/issue-brief/trapped-workers-a-network-analysis-of-worker-mobility-in-the-ai-economy/).
  🟡 (descriptive network/mobility-pattern analysis over observed worker transitions, not a causal
  or experimental design; centrist think tank, no obvious commercial conflict, but a model-based
  exposure classification with self-acknowledged limits). *Shows:* using 595,000 observed US
  worker occupation-to-occupation transitions (2019–2026), the authors map 16 occupational
  mobility clusters and ask not just "is this occupation AI-exposed?" but "where do workers in
  this occupation actually go next, and is *that* destination also exposed?" — distinguishing
  "escapable" (exposed role, but typical next-step roles are lower-exposure — e.g. medical
  transcriptionists) from "trapped" (typical next-step roles are similarly exposed — e.g. tax
  preparers moving into other exposed administrative roles). The share classified "trapped" is
  **highly scenario-dependent**: 10 of 31 highly-exposed occupations (32%) under a "moderate"
  (routine-tasks-only) exposure scenario vs 58 of 85 (68%) under an "aggressive" (complex-
  cognitive-work-included) scenario — press coverage citing "two in three trapped" is quoting only
  the aggressive scenario, not a single settled figure. Authors' own limits: mobility patterns are
  backward-looking (2019–2026 observed transitions) and don't account for new roles AI might
  create or for augmentation (vs. replacement) workflows. *Why it matters for the meaning
  project:* this is a genuinely new **methodological lens** on the "does the 'new jobs' mechanism
  hold?" watchlist question (MANIFESTO §analogy breakpoints) — it operationalizes "can a displaced
  worker actually land somewhere" as an empirical mobility-network property rather than an
  assumption, complementing the exposure-level literature already in evidence_base.md (Stanford/
  ADP, ECB, PwC, Census BTOS). It doesn't yet clear the bar for a 🟢 evidence_base entry (no causal
  identification, scenario-sensitive headline number, single think-tank release) — flagging here
  as a 🟡 item to watch for independent replication or a peer-reviewed version. → not yet a
  candidate for evidence_base; revisit if replicated or if BPC releases the companion "Trapped
  Workers: Who AI Leaves Behind" brief's full methodology.
