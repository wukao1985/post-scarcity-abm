## Items

- **"Loneliness predicts mortality risk via the erosion of purpose in life"** — Emile,
  *Social Science & Medicine* 368 (Jan 23, 2026).
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0277953626000833
  🟢 (prospective empirical). Health and Retirement Study, N = 8,351 US adults, 11-year
  follow-up. *What it actually shows:* purpose in life statistically explains ~88% of the
  loneliness → premature mortality association; the mediation runs primarily through
  *changes* in purpose over time, not baseline purpose alone; robust to adjustment for
  depression, social isolation, and neuroticism. *Limits:* observational, older adult
  sample (mean age ~68), so causality and generalisability to working-age populations
  require care. *Why it matters:* this is the clearest 🟢 evidence yet that "purpose
  erosion" is not a vague cultural concern but a measurable mediator of a mortality
  outcome — directly grounding Q3 (can AI supply "mattering"?), the care/relatedness
  cluster, and the "meaning as health variable" framing in the writing. Pairs with the
  human-empathy preference findings already in evidence_base.
  → candidate for evidence_base (purpose-erosion / mortality mechanism).
  → idea: "purpose is a vital sign" — the mortality data makes the case that policy
  framing meaning-loss as a soft cultural issue is miscalibrated; it is measurable and
  lethal at population scale.

- **IMF Staff Discussion Note SDN/2026/001, "Bridging Skill Gaps for the Future: New Jobs
  Creation in the AI Age"** — Goraya, Mendes Tavares et al., IMF (Jan 9, 2026).
  https://www.imf.org/en/publications/staff-discussion-notes/issues/2026/01/09/bridging-skill-gaps-for-the-future-new-jobs-creation-in-the-ai-age-572136
  🟢 (government empirical report; job-postings data). *What it actually shows:* new-skill
  job postings carry a 2–3.4% wage premium (US/UK) and a 1.3pp employment gain in local
  markets — *but* AI-specific skill postings carry higher wages (~7.5–8% UK) with **no
  overall employment gain** in local US labour markets; middle-skilled workers capture no
  significant benefit; polarisation (high + low skill) is reinforced. *Limits:* job
  postings ≠ filled jobs; Lightcast coverage skews toward formal employment. *Why it
  matters:* the "new jobs mechanism" held in prior waves (general new skills → more jobs)
  but for AI-specific skills it holds only at the wage level, not the employment level —
  and leaves the middle out. This is the empirical basis for saying "AI creates winners at
  the top and doesn't lift the middle." Completes the watchlist item flagged 2026-05-31.
  → pairs with the Stanford/ADP young-cohort finding already in evidence_base (same
  story from a different angle: young + middle exposed but not gaining).

## Watchlist movement

- **Automation vs augmentation (analogy breakpoint #1) — potential flip to watch:**
  Yale Budget Lab's April 16, 2026 tracking report, citing Anthropic's February 2026
  usage data, states that Claude usage is now "more likely to be associated with
  automation than augmentation." This *potentially* reverses the 52% augmentation /
  45% automation split recorded in the evidence_base from the November 2025 Anthropic
  Economic Index (5th report). *Caveat:* Yale's framing may differ from Anthropic's
  own report methodology (e.g., API vs. consumer weighting); the November 2025 Anthropic
  report already noted "automation briefly led in Aug 2025" and "API skews automated."
  Do NOT treat this as confirmed until the underlying Anthropic data release is read in
  primary. **Flag:** if automation has genuinely overtaken augmentation in aggregate
  Claude usage, this is a significant analogy-breakpoint movement — the project's
  cautiously optimistic "augmentation-dominant" characterisation would need updating.
  Source: https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/tracking-impact-ai-labor-market
