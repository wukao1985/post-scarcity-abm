## Items

- **"We Must Act Now": a Nobel-laureate-backed economist statement on AI's economic transformation**
  — Stanford Digital Economy Lab (organized by Erik Brynjolfsson, Ajay Agrawal, Anton Korinek, Tom
  Cunningham), released 13 Jul 2026 (wemustactnow.ai; primary org page verified). 🟡 (a signed
  expert-consensus policy statement, not empirical data — explicitly frames outcomes as *not
  predetermined*, contingent on policy choice, not a prediction). *Shows:* 200+ economists and AI
  researchers, including **16 Nobel laureates**, signed a short statement warning AI could bring an
  economic transformation "larger than the Industrial Revolution... arriving much faster" — explicit
  risk named is **large-scale job displacement** alongside possible living-standard gains; calls for
  deepened research and institutions/policy to keep AI complementing rather than displacing workers.
  Notable: co-signers include **Daron Acemoglu and Simon Johnson** (2024 Nobel laureates), reported by
  multiple outlets as having previously been *skeptical* of AI-displacement alarm — their signing reads
  as a shift in the credentialed-economist consensus, not new data. *Limits:* a values/advocacy
  statement, not a study — cites no new dataset, and by design takes no position on whether
  displacement *will* happen, only that the stakes and pace warrant preparation now. *Why it matters
  for the project:* directly relevant to the "speed vs. institutional adjustment" watchlist variable
  (the ILO J-curve entry, Mertens/MIT "rising tides" pace finding) — this is the clearest institutional
  signal yet that mainstream (not fringe) economists now treat the *pace* of AI change, not just its
  eventual scale, as the live risk. Organized by the same Stanford lab behind the already-anchored
  ADP/Brynjolfsson "Canaries in the Coal Mine" young-cohort finding, so it is a credible extension of
  a source this project already trusts, not a new/unvetted one. *Honesty flag:* this is a catch-up,
  not a same-week find — it was missed by the 2026-07-14, -15, and -16 daily scans despite wide
  coverage (Axios, NBC, ABC, The Hill) the same week it broke; logging it now rather than leaving the
  gap, in the same spirit as the UN Scientific Panel and Ramp/Revelio catches earlier this month. →
  worth a line in the watchlist/synthesis as institutional-consensus texture (🟡), not a 🟢 anchor.
