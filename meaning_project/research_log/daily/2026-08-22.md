## Items

- **"Interaction with AI Companions and Psychological Well-Being"** — Zhang, Zhao, Hancock, Kraut
  & Yang (Stanford), *Nature Human Behaviour*, published ~4 Aug 2026
  (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02516-2; read via Stanford HAI/Stanford Report
  primary coverage, not the Nature full text). 🟢 (peer-reviewed; survey N=1,131 Character.AI
  users via Prolific, with 244 donating full chat transcripts coded by GPT-4o/LLaMA-3-70B/TopicGPT
  for engagement patterns; well-being via the Comprehensive Inventory of Thriving) **with caveats**
  — correlational, self-selected Prolific/Character.AI sample, no effect sizes reported in the
  secondary sources I could reach, cross-sectional (no baseline/panel). *Shows:* intense chatbot
  use correlates with *lower* well-being specifically among users with **small offline social
  networks** who use the bot **for companionship** and **disclose sensitive information** to it —
  the opposite of what self-disclosure predicts in human relationships; researchers frame this as
  a "social snack" (short-term relief, no durable nourishment). Only ~12% of users state
  companionship as their primary motive, yet >80% of chat content is emotional-support-seeking and
  >50% call the bot "friend"/"companion" — a stated-vs-revealed-preference gap. *Why it matters:*
  this is a new, methodologically distinct (survey + coded transcript, not just self-report or
  Reddit-language proxy) addition to the Q3 relatedness cluster (Aalto/Replika, Folk & Dunn 12-mo
  panel, Li et al. RCT) — same direction, and it specifies the mechanism further: harm concentrates
  in the *already-isolated*, not universally. *Honesty note:* this was **not caught within the
  24–48h window** — it published ~18 days ago and slipped past every daily scan from 2026-08-04
  through 2026-08-21 (confirmed via grep across that whole span — no prior mention). Flagging it
  now as a late catch, the same way the UN Scientific Panel report was caught late in 2026-W28,
  rather than leaving it permanently missed. → candidate for evidence_base (Q3 relatedness
  cluster); primary Nature Human Behaviour text should be read directly before final wording if
  this becomes an article citation, since I only reached secondary/institutional coverage today.
