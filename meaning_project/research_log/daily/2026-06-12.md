## Items

- **"Do Job Postings Show Early Labor-Market Effects of AI?"** — Richard Audoly, Miles Guerin
  & Giorgio Topa; *Liberty Street Economics* (Federal Reserve Bank of New York), 14 May 2026.
  https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/05/do-job-postings-show-early-labor-market-effects-of-ai/
  🟢 (Federal Reserve economists; primary job-postings dataset analysis; blog-post format rather
  than peer-reviewed journal article — treat as staff analysis, not a settled finding; US-only;
  limited post-2022 window; Lightcast job-postings data skews toward formal advertised roles).
  *What it actually shows:* Analysis of job-postings data grouped by AI occupational exposure.
  High-exposure occupations do show **relatively lower vacancies** — but the divergence began
  **before** the November 2022 ChatGPT release, predating the generative-AI era. Separately,
  there is **no divergence between junior and senior job postings** within highly exposed
  occupations, which complicates the "AI is selectively closing the entry-level door" reading
  that the Stanford/ADP paper and Massenkoff & McCrory (March 2026) suggested. NY Fed
  business surveys indicate firms plan to incorporate AI mainly through **retraining**, not
  hiring cuts. Headline conclusion: "while overall hiring has slowed, the evidence from job
  postings provides little indication of a distinct AI-driven decline in labor demand." Also
  notes: fewer than 10% of US workers and vacancies are in occupations with AI exposure ≥ 0.4;
  40% are in roles with zero measured exposure. *Limits (state honestly):* blog-post format;
  Lightcast data does not capture informal or gig labour; the "divergence predates ChatGPT"
  finding depends on which AI-exposure index is used; firm survey data on "retraining intent"
  is prospective and self-reported; the junior/senior null contradicts Massenkoff & McCrory's
  hiring-rate-narrowing finding and may reflect different data sources or index choices — the
  two results are in genuine tension, not yet resolved. *Why it matters:* This is now the
  fourth institutional null or complication on the AI-attribution question — after the
  project's own contract-buffer null (CPS microdata), Emanuel/Harrington/Pallais (remote-work
  confound, NY Fed, June 2026, in evidence_base), and Massenkoff & McCrory's "no unemployment
  stock signal" (March 2026, in evidence_base). The "divergence predates ChatGPT" finding is
  the strongest version of the attribution sceptic case yet: the relative decline in high-
  exposure vacancies may be a secular occupational trend, not a generative-AI effect. For the
  project's labour watchlist, the honest position is now: something is happening in high-
  exposure occupations, but the causal story is genuinely contested at the level of timing,
  magnitude, and mechanism. The junior/senior null, if replicated, directly complicates the
  "entry-level door is already closing" thread that the Stanford/ADP paper anchors. Worth
  tracking against the next BLS occupational employment update.

- **"Policy on the AI Exponential"** — Dario Amodei; personal website (darioamodei.com),
  published ~10 June 2026. Accompanied by Anthropic's $200M Economic Futures Research Fund
  + $150M early-career fellowship announcement (10 June 2026).
  https://darioamodei.com/post/policy-on-the-ai-exponential
  🔴 (normative advocacy — an AI company CEO proposing policy; not empirical; prominent
  conflict of interest: Anthropic both creates AI and is commercially invested in its adoption;
  treat as a policy-discourse signal, not a data point).
  *What it actually shows:* A sweeping policy essay spanning frontier model regulation, labour
  displacement, civil liberties, biomedical acceleration, and geopolitics. On labour: Amodei
  states enduring job displacement is "undesirable and dangerous" and proposes an escalating
  government response keyed to three unemployment thresholds (5%, 10%, and higher). The policy
  ladder runs: **wage insurance** → employer retention incentives → training grants → at extreme
  displacement levels, **long-term income support / UBI financed by taxes on AI companies or
  higher capital gains taxes**. Fortune's framing (11 June): "Anthropic just proposed taxing
  itself to pay for the jobs its AI destroys." The $200M Economic Futures Research Fund is
  described as backing research trials and program evaluations on promising policies; the $150M
  fellowship targets early-career professionals. *Limits (state honestly):* advocacy from an
  interested party; Anthropic has obvious incentives to appear responsible without slowing
  deployment; the unemployment-threshold framework is a thought experiment, not enacted
  legislation; wage insurance and displacement-tax proposals are not new ideas; the specific
  thresholds (5%, 10%) are arbitrary choices; none of the $350M has yet produced evidence; the
  essay does not address the meaning/purpose dimension of displacement — only the income
  replacement layer. *Why it matters:* The MANIFESTO identifies "serious futures/policy
  thinking" as a scan category. The signal here is the **genre shift**: the CEO of a
  front-rank AI lab is publicly proposing UBI financed by AI company taxes as a contingency
  for large-scale displacement, not dismissing the risk. For the industry, this is a discourse
  landmark. For the writing, it is 🔴 illustration material, not evidence: the fact that even
  the builder is proposing tax-funded UBI as a contingency implicitly concedes that (a) large
  displacement is plausible, and (b) spontaneous re-employment and "find meaning elsewhere"
  adjustment will not automatically close the gap. The essay's income-only framing also
  sharpens the project's niche: Amodei has nothing to say about *meaning* — his framework
  addresses cash, not purpose, identity, or the psychological architecture of post-work life.
  That gap is exactly where this project sits.
  → idea: "what the builder left out" — Amodei's essay is detailed on income support and
  silent on the psychological stakes; the income-only framing of even the most thoughtful
  industry proposal is itself evidence that the meaning layer is the neglected question.

## Watchlist movement

- **Attribution of labour decline (breakpoint #1 — what is being automated, and does
  the "new jobs" mechanism hold):** The NY Fed job-postings paper is the strongest challenge
  yet to the AI-causation reading of the high-exposure vacancy decline: the divergence predates
  ChatGPT. This does not close the question — secular AI-related trends began before 2022, and
  the causal attribution for any particular post-2022 acceleration remains live — but the
  honest position is now that the macro labour signal is contested at the level of *timing* as
  well as magnitude. Monitor the junior/senior vacancy divergence (or lack of it) across future
  datasets; the Massenkoff & McCrory and Audoly et al. findings are in tension and need
  reconciliation.
- **Discourse-level — AI company policy positioning:** Amodei essay marks a public-discourse
  shift worth tracking: if the leading labs are now pre-emptively proposing tax-funded UBI
  as a displacement contingency, watch whether this accelerates legislative movement on wage
  insurance or UBI pilots — which would, in turn, create new 🟢 evaluation opportunities.
