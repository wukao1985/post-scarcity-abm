## Items

- **Dixon/YouGov survey: self-reported AI job impact among employed US workers** — reported via
  TechXplore, 27 Aug 2026 (primary study by sociologist Jeffrey C. Dixon, unpublished/in
  progress, not peer-reviewed). 🟡 (single academic-commissioned online survey via YouGov,
  N=1,250 employed US workers, fielded 30 Jul–4 Aug 2026; author's own caveats: self-report/
  misattribution risk, self-selected online panel, employed-only sample excludes the jobless,
  fielded during a shrinking-workforce period, preliminary/unpublished). *Shows:* only ~3% of
  currently-employed respondents attribute a past job loss (since 2023) to AI automation, ~6%
  report having obtained an AI-created job, ~9% report an AI-skills-related promotion — i.e. among
  people who currently have a job, self-reported AI displacement is small, contra the volume of
  AI-layoff headlines. Why it matters: it's a data point on the *attribution* debate this project
  already tracks (Massenkoff & McCrory's no-unemployment-stock-rise, Census BTOS, the Yotzov
  employer/worker expectations gap, and the *rejected* Resume Genius survey of laid-off workers
  where 53% blamed AI) — this is the mirror-image population (the still-employed) reporting a much
  lower self-attributed rate, consistent with "AI risk is concentrated and narrow, not broadly
  felt across the currently-employed," but it is unpublished, self-report, and from a single
  academic survey commissioned via a market-research panel, so it does not clear the bar for the
  evidence base — logged as a data point, not a candidate anchor. Worth a primary-source check
  once/if Dixon's study is published.
