## Items

- **Bank of Korea — "Is Youth Employment Contraction Due to AI? Changing Career Ladders and
  Response Tasks"** (청년고용 위축, AI탓인가? 변화하는 경력 사다리와 대응 과제), BOK Research
  Department Employment Research Team, BOK Issue Note, released 18 Aug 2026. Read via multiple
  Korean financial-press outlets (Korea Times, Korea JoongAng Daily, Newstomato, Herald Corp,
  e-focus, CBC News Korea) that consistently attribute the same figures and title to the same BOK
  report; I could not retrieve the primary BOK PDF/URL directly (only the institution's general
  Issue Note index page), so this is 🟡 **institutional-via-secondary**, not a directly-verified
  🟢. Methodology: Korean employment statistics, industries classified by AI-exposure quartile
  (info services, publishing, computer programming/systems, professional services = high-exposure),
  June 2022–June 2026 window (ChatGPT launch as reference start). *Shows:* youth (15–29) employment
  fell 285,000 over the 4 years; **268,000 (94.0%) of that loss was concentrated in high-AI-exposure
  sectors** (info services −31.4%, publishing −27.4%, computer programming −16.6%, professional
  services −11.6%), while workers 50+ gained 230,000 jobs over the same window (173,000, ~75%, in
  the same high-exposure sectors) — a near-mirror generational divergence. Youth share of all new
  hires fell from a 2014–2019 pre-pandemic average of 36.9% to 29.3% in 2024–2026. **Notably, the
  BOK's own conclusion is hedged, not alarmist:** the report states AI is better understood as
  "accelerating or amplifying pre-existing changes in hiring and work patterns" than as the
  "culprit" of youth employment contraction — the authors do not claim a clean causal
  identification. *Why it matters:* this is the first **South Korean** institutional data point in
  this base on the young/old AI-exposed employment divergence — the same shape as the Stanford/ADP
  "Canaries" young-cohort gap (19%, US, ADP payroll data) and the CRR older-worker mirror (US, CPS),
  now replicated in a structurally different labour market (Korea's seniority-wage system, national
  pension enrollment as the underlying data source) with the same generational-inversion pattern
  and the same honest caveat (correlational, not a clean natural experiment) that already accompanies
  the US findings. Strengthens the international generality of the "AI-exposed career edges fraying"
  thread without overclaiming causation — the BOK's own hedge is worth preserving verbatim if this
  becomes a citation. → candidate for evidence_base (international companion to the Stanford/ADP +
  CRR entry-level/older-worker cluster); primary BOK PDF should be located and read directly before
  any article citation, since this scan only reached secondary Korean-press coverage.
