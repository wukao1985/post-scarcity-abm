## Items

- **"Mental Health Impacts of AI Companions: Triangulating Social Media Quasi-Experiments,
  User Perspectives, and Relational Theory"** — Aalto University researchers; *Proceedings
  of the CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems 2026*.
  https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790558 (ACM DL) /
  https://www.aalto.fi/en/news/ai-companions-can-comfort-lonely-users-but-may-deepen-distress-over-time (press release)
  🟢 (quasi-experimental, large-N observational; not pre-registered as far as known;
  single-platform — Reddit/Replika — so generalisation across AI companions requires care).
  *What it actually shows:* Analysed the public Reddit language of ~2,000 active Replika
  users, comparing posts one year *before* and one year *after* each user first mentioned
  using the AI companion, against matched comparison groups. Short-term comfort plausible,
  but the long-term picture reverses: users' posts showed *more* loneliness, depression,
  and suicidal-ideation signals over time than comparison groups. Identified mechanism: AI
  companions "quietly raise the perceived cost of human relationships" — messy, effortful
  human connection becomes comparatively costly relative to the always-available AI, causing
  users to stop reaching out. Long-term use associated with *reduced* engagement with human
  relationships, not increased. *Limits (state honestly):* observational quasi-experiment
  (not randomised), so confounding is possible (e.g., users in deepening distress may seek
  AI companions more — reverse causality); single platform (Replika) may not generalise to
  other AI companions or use cases; Reddit language is a proxy for internal states.
  *Why it matters:* This is the clearest behavioural-level test yet of whether AI can
  substitute for human relatedness — the direct empirical counterpart to the "people prefer
  human empathy" study already in evidence_base. The empathy-preference study shows that AI
  *labelled as AI* is valued less than identical human responses; this study shows what
  happens *behaviourally* when lonely people turn to AI for emotional support over time:
  a crowd-out of human connection, not its replacement. Together they ground a concrete
  answer to Q3 ("can AI supply mattering?"): the evidence leans no — AI companionship is
  not a substitute for relatedness and may actively worsen the deficit by shifting users'
  social cost calculus. Policy implication worth naming: AI companions are being deployed
  by governments and health systems as loneliness interventions; this data suggests the
  intervention may backfire at scale if it substitutes for, rather than bridges to, human
  connection.
  → candidate for evidence_base (AI companion crowd-out effect; pairs with human-empathy
  preference study; grounds Q3 relatedness path).
  → idea: "the relatedness trap" — piece arguing that AI companions, marketed as a loneliness
  solution, may structurally worsen the problem by raising the perceived cost of human
  relationship effort; the policy recommendation reverses: AI companions as *bridges* to
  human connection, not endpoints — and how you design the system determines which it is.
