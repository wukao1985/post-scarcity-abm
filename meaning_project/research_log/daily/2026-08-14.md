## Items

- **Stanford Digital Economy Lab × ADP Research — "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" August 2026
  revision (Brynjolfsson, Collis, Lee, Wang, Nguyen & Kazinnik)** — published 12 Aug 2026
  ([Stanford Digital Economy Lab](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)).
  🟢 (institutional press page + publication page verified directly; the full primary working-
  paper PDF was fetched but its text could not be extracted this session — treat the specific
  numbers below as read from Stanford's own summary of the paper, not independently parsed from
  the PDF). **What it shows, precisely:** this is a revision of the project's already-anchored
  "single clearest 🟢 signal of labor harm" (young 22–25 workers in AI-exposed occupations losing
  ground), extending the ADP payroll data from July 2025 through **June 2026**. The relative
  employment gap for 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations widened to **~19%** (from
  ~16% in the Nov-2025 version), with an absolute decline of **~11%** for the two most-exposed
  quintiles vs **~+10%** growth for similarly-aged workers in less-exposed occupations
  (Nov-2022→Jun-2026). The adjustment mechanism remains **reduced hiring**, not increased
  separations, concentrated in occupations where AI **substitutes for** rather than augments
  human tasks. **The genuinely new part:** the revision explicitly re-runs the test against the
  confound this project flagged in 2026-W23 — the NY Fed's Emanuel/Harrington/Pallais finding
  that remote work, not AI, explains ~two-thirds of the same young-grad unemployment rise. The
  authors report the AI-exposed gap "remains when we exclude technology firms and computer
  occupations, when we control for exposure to interest-rate increases and **remote work**, when
  we include firms that enter or leave the sample, and when we use alternative measures of AI
  exposure." **Why it matters for the project:** this doesn't settle the AI-vs-remote-work
  dispute (no regression coefficient or effect size is given for the remote-work-controlled
  specification in what I could verify — only that the gap "remains"), but it is the paper's own
  authors directly engaging the exact confound this project logged as unresolved, on updated
  data, with the AI-attributable divergence widening rather than narrowing. Strengthens the
  young-cohort anchor and narrows (without closing) the attribution dispute. → **folded into
  evidence_base** (extended the existing Stanford/ADP entry with the Aug-2026 figures and the
  remote-work-control result). → idea: worth a dedicated primary-PDF read (once text extraction
  is possible) to check whether the remote-work-controlled coefficient is reported with a
  magnitude, before treating this as resolving rather than merely narrowing the confound.
  https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/
