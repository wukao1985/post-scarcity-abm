## Items

- **Ramp Economics Lab × Revelio Labs — "A New Look at AI's Impact on Jobs": firm-level AI
  *spending* linked to workforce records across 21,599 US firms.** Ramp / Revelio Labs, released
  30 Jun 2026 (ramp.com/data/ai-jobs-impact; reveliolabs.com). 🟢 **with a prominent caveat** —
  real transaction data (Ramp corporate-card/bill-pay payments to AI vendors, e.g. OpenAI,
  Anthropic) matched to real workforce panel data (Revelio), not a survey or capability proxy —
  but Ramp is a commercially interested party (a corporate-card/fintech firm with incentive to
  show AI spending correlates with growth), and the authors themselves flag standard selection
  bias. *Shows:* firms in the top third of AI spending-per-employee ("high-intensity adopters")
  grew total headcount ~10.2% and entry-level headcount ~12% over the two years after adoption,
  while the bottom two-thirds ("low-intensity adopters") saw no statistically significant
  employment change (and a modest *decline* in entry-level share). Authors explicitly state:
  "AI adopters are not a random sample of employers" — they were already larger, more
  engineering-intensive, more venture-backed, and faster-growing before adopting — so this is
  correlational, not a causal test, and the lead economist called it "early results ... we will
  update over time." *Why it matters:* this is the first **deployment-spend-linked** (not
  exposure-modelled, not self-report) large-sample test of the core "does AI investment cost
  jobs" question, and it runs *against* the layoff narrative, including at the entry level —
  sitting in unresolved tension with the already-anchored Census BTOS finding that Information and
  Finance/Insurance (the two sectors this project tracks *declining* on payrolls) are also the two
  adopting AI *fastest*. Both can be true simultaneously (aggregate-adoption correlating with
  sector decline; firm-level-spend-intensity correlating with firm-level growth) if AI spending is
  itself a marker of already-fast-growing firms displacing slower-growing competitors within a
  shrinking sector — but that reconciliation is speculative, not tested by either study. *Honesty
  flag:* this predates the 24–48h window by about two weeks; it does not appear in any daily log
  from 2026-07-01 through 2026-07-13 (checked directly), so it looks like a genuine miss by prior
  scans rather than a considered exclusion — logging it now rather than leaving the gap, in the
  same spirit as the UN Independent Scientific Panel catch on 2026-07-13. → candidate for
  evidence_base (pairs with / complicates the Census BTOS adoption-gradient entry and the Goldman
  Sachs payroll decomposition; needs its own line under "Productivity / labour-market effects").
