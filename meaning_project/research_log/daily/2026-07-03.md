## Items

- **Bloomberg — "Tech and Finance Sectors Losing 28,000 Jobs Monthly Show AI Impact on Labor"**
  (Bloomberg, 1 Jul 2026; syndicated via Insurance Journal 2 Jul 2026;
  https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-01/tech-and-finance-sectors-losing-28-000-jobs-monthly-show-ai-impact-on-labor).
  🟡 (journalism synthesizing several sources) **built on one 🟢 primary component** — the
  Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey (BTOS) AI-use supplement, verified
  directly at census.gov: as of the 3 May 2026 collection wave, Information-sector AI use is
  39.7% and Finance/Insurance 33.9%, both against a 19.8% national average — the first
  sector-level AI-*adoption-rate* figures (not exposure/capability proxies) this log has
  logged. *What it actually shows:* financial-activities + information payrolls (BLS data)
  have declined ~28,000/month on average through May 2026, even as the broader economy added
  ~113,000 jobs/month over the same period — consistent with, and updating, the project's
  existing financial (−107K/12mo) and information (−342K since Nov-22) sector tracking.
  Separately, a **Ramp/Revelio Labs** study (21,559 US companies, Jan 2021–Feb 2026, cited in
  the piece) found companies with **high-intensity AI spending grew headcount 10.2% over two
  years, including 12% entry-level hiring growth**, while low-intensity adopters saw no
  significant change — task automation correlates with the job losses, augmentation-heavy
  adopters are hiring more, including at entry level. *Why it matters:* two new data points
  for the augmentation-vs-automation watchlist. The Census BTOS adoption-by-sector figures are
  a genuinely new, verifiable 🟢 anchor for "which sectors have adopted AI fastest" (distinct
  from capability-exposure indices used elsewhere in the evidence base). The Ramp/Revelio
  finding is a useful **counterweight** to the Stanford/ADP young-cohort and PwC "raised floor"
  entry-level-harm readings — at the *firm* level, heavy AI adopters are hiring more entry-level
  workers, not fewer; this doesn't overturn the occupation-level findings (different unit of
  analysis — firm AI-spending intensity vs. occupational AI-exposure) but is a real tension
  worth carrying forward. *Limits to state honestly:* the Census figures are solid (BTOS is a
  federal survey, ~50% response rate range typical for BTOS, self-reported "used AI in the past
  two weeks" — a low bar that doesn't distinguish trivial from transformative use); the
  Ramp/Revelio study is private/proprietary (Ramp sells expense-management software; the sample
  is not necessarily representative — likely skews toward Ramp's own customer base, itself an
  AI-adopting-firm-leaning sample), so its "no bias" cannot be fully assessed from this summary;
  the Bloomberg piece's own economists note "it remains too early to detect broad effects" at
  the macro level. *Not a new evidence-base pillar on its own* — but the Census BTOS
  sector-adoption figures are worth citing directly (census.gov) rather than via the Bloomberg
  wrapper if used in writing. → candidate for evidence_base as a *sourcing correction*: replace/
  supplement existing sector-decline citations with the primary Census BTOS adoption-rate data.
