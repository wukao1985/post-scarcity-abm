## The week in one paragraph

**A structurally quiet week whose one signal was a late catch on the project's strongest thread.** After
W33's double durability-test (German RCT persistence; Stanford/ADP widening to 19% and surviving a
remote-work control), the aggregate machinery went quiet: no new jobs report (the next hard aggregate point,
the August Employment Situation, is not due until early September), no new NBER/arXiv empirical labour paper
(only already-anchored w33509/w33777/w34836/w34984 and theoretical pieces resurfacing), the AI-cited layoffs
tracker frozen at its running figure (322 events / 205,832 workers / 170,945 citing AI, ~903→883/day as the
denominator's date range lengthens — no new inflection since Aug 6), and the recurring SDT-and-AI conference
cluster (*Behaviors* 16(5):670, ECIS 2026, the *Frontiers* "dual-path" and PLOS One mediation papers) again
recapitulating Lee et al./ILO WP170 without new causal identification (rejected continuously since Jul 20).
Into that silence, one item that actually matters: **Zhang, Zhao, Hancock, Kraut & Yang (Stanford), *Nature
Human Behaviour***, a survey of 1,131 US Character.AI users with 237 of them donating 4,664 chat sessions
(≈464,687 messages) coded for engagement patterns. Its finding rhymes with the project's existing
relatedness cluster (Aalto/Replika, Folk & Dunn's 12-month panel, Li et al.'s RCT) — intense, disclosive,
companionship-motivated chatbot use tracks *lower* well-being — but it **sharpens the causal story in a way
the anchors could not**: the harm is *conditional*, concentrated among users with **small offline social
networks** who use the bot **for companionship** and **disclose sensitive material** to it, while users who
came for **productivity or curiosity report higher** well-being. Zhang's own gloss — a "**social snack**":
appealing short-term relief with none of the ingredients of durable connection — is the cleanest one-line
statement yet of the "AI companions target the wrong variable" reading the project has been building since
W23. Two honest caveats keep it from being over-read: it is **cross-sectional and correlational** (no
baseline, no panel — self-selected Prolific/Character.AI users, so the isolated may simply *seek* companion
bots), and the specifics here are read from **Stanford's own report + the arXiv preprint (2506.12605)**, not
the paywalled *Nature* full text — no controlled effect size could be verified. So: not a new pillar, but a
methodologically distinct fourth leg under the project's strongest sub-question, and the one thing an
otherwise silent week produced. The rest of the week was the discipline of *not* logging — a fabricated-
looking loneliness/frailty statistic rejected twice for want of a source, and a dozen recirculating items
correctly identified as already-anchored.

  Character.AI users recruited via Prolific, with **237** donating **4,664** chat sessions / ≈**464,687**
  messages coded by GPT-4o/LLaMA-3-70B/TopicGPT for engagement patterns; well-being via the Comprehensive
  Inventory of Thriving) — but **cross-sectional and correlational**, self-selected sample, no baseline/panel,
  and read here from **Stanford Report + arXiv preprint, not the paywalled *Nature* full text** (no effect
  sizes verified). *Shows:* the well-being association is **conditional, not uniform** — intense chatbot use
  correlates with *lower* well-being specifically among users with **small offline social networks** who use
  the bot **for companionship** and **disclose sensitive information** to it (the opposite of what self-
  disclosure predicts between humans), while users motivated by **productivity or curiosity report higher**
  well-being. A stated-vs-revealed-preference gap runs through it: only ~**12%** name companionship as their
  primary motive, yet >**80%** of chat content is emotional-support-seeking and >**50%** call the bot
  "friend"/"companion." Zhang's framing: a "**social snack**" — short-term relief, no durable nourishment.
  *Significance for the meaning niche:* a **methodologically distinct fourth leg** under Q3 (the project's
  best-evidenced sub-question), and the first to use **coded transcripts** rather than Reddit-language proxy
  (Aalto) or bare self-report (Folk & Dunn, Li et al.). Same *direction* as the cluster, and it **localises**
  the harm — concentrated in the *already-isolated*, the very people the "companion-as-cure" pitch targets —
  which is exactly the mechanism the "AI companions target the wrong variable" coinage predicts. → **folded
  into evidence_base** (Q3 relatedness cluster). Follow-up: read the primary *Nature* text before any article
  citation, to recover effect sizes and confirm the 237/4,664/464,687 figures against the published version.
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02516-2 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12605 ·
