## The week in one paragraph

**The week two anchors got a durability test — and both passed.** For three-and-a-half weeks the log had
been reporting an unbroken quiet run (the July jobs report in W32 was the last dated item, and it confirmed
the null). W33 broke it on the 13th and 14th, and the striking thing is that the two items rhyme. The
**German basic-income RCT** (Bohmann, Fiedler, Kasy, Schupp & Schwerter, CESifo WP 11989, 23-Mar-2026
revision — verified from the primary PDF) added two post-program follow-up waves and showed that **91% of
its during-program wellbeing gain persisted 18 months after the final transfer**: income satisfaction fades
(0.68 SD in-program → 0.13 SD, non-significant, at 18mo post) exactly as hedonic-adaptation theory predicts,
while **mental health, life satisfaction, and purpose in life hold at 0.28–0.31 SD long after the payments
stopped.** That is a materially stronger claim than the anchor previously carried ("stable *while the money
flows*") — it is durability *after the money stops*, and it directly contradicts the contemporaneous US RCT
(Miller/Vivalt/Bartik) that found no durable mental-health effect at all, with the authors nominating
*individual (vs household-shared) discretionary control* over the transfer as the operative hinge. The next
day, **Stanford's "Canaries in the Coal Mine?"** (12-Aug revision; Brynjolfsson, Collis, Lee, Wang, Nguyen &
Kazinnik) extended the ADP payroll data through June 2026 and updated the project's "single clearest 🟢
signal of labor harm" — the 22–25-year-old AI-exposed employment gap — from ~16% to **~19%**, and, crucially,
reported that the divergence **"remains when we control for … remote work,"** engaging head-on the NY Fed
remote-work confound this project flagged in W23 as unresolved. So the week's real content is not "new
findings" but **two existing anchors surviving the sharpest challenge each faces**: does the cash-transfer
purpose gain outlast the cash? (yes) and is the young-cohort harm really AI rather than remote work? (the
signal widened and survived the control). The discipline note still applies to both: the German result is
still N=107, self-report, single-country; the Stanford remote-work-control result is read from the press/
publication summary, not the primary regression, so it **narrows but does not close** the attribution dispute
— no coefficient or magnitude for the controlled specification could be verified. Two anchors strengthened,
neither over-claimed.

## Notable items

- **Bohmann, Fiedler, Kasy, Schupp & Schwerter — "Cash Transfers, Mental Health, and Agency: Evidence from
  an RCT in Germany"** — CESifo WP No. 11989 (this version 23 Mar 2026; original Jul 2025), verified from the
  primary PDF (ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp11989.pdf). Logged in the Aug-13 daily. 🟢 (same pre-registered RCT
  already anchored — €1,200/mo for 3 yr, N=107 treated / 1,580 control, AEA RCT Registry #7734 — but this
  revision adds a **new wave of results** not previously captured). *Shows:* two post-program follow-up waves
  (6 and 18 months *after* the final transfer, through ~Nov 2025). The mental-health-and-wellbeing index was
  **0.379 SD in-program → 0.331 SD at 6mo post → 0.362 SD at 18mo post** — **91% persistence after the money
  stopped.** Income satisfaction fades to non-significance (ordinary hedonic adaptation to the lost income
  stream); **mental health, life/health/sleep satisfaction, and purpose in life hold at 0.28–0.31 SD.**
  Authors' account: durable *life changes* (saved ~⅓ of the transfer, +1.3 hrs/week with friends, higher
  perceived autonomy +0.32 SD) that became self-sustaining — an *exploratory*, not causally-identified,
  mechanism. *Significance for the meaning niche:* the sharpest evidence yet against reading cash-transfer
  wellbeing gains as merely "relief while the money flows." It deepens the project's core UBI synthesis and
  directly sharpens the **"Money buys relief, not meaning"** coinage: the *purpose* component is now shown to
  be not just present but *durable*, while the *relief* component fades — the exact opposite of the naive
  read. The individual-vs-household discretionary-control divergence from the US RCT is a **testable,
  falsifiable hinge** worth tracking against future high-income-country pilots. *Limits (honest):* N=107,
  single country, self-report, non-representative (single-person households, below-average income, age
  21–40); a revision of an already-partially-anchored study, not a new one. → **folded into evidence_base**
  (German RCT entry extended with the 18mo post-program persistence figures, tagged 2026-W33).
  https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp11989.pdf

- **Stanford Digital Economy Lab × ADP Research — "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" August 2026 revision**
  (Brynjolfsson, Collis, Lee, Wang, Nguyen & Kazinnik) — published 12 Aug 2026, logged in the Aug-14 daily.
  🟢 (institutional press + publication pages verified directly; the specific numbers are read from Stanford's
  own summary — the full primary working-paper PDF was fetched but text extraction failed this session).
  *Shows:* a revision of the project's already-anchored "single clearest 🟢 signal of labor harm" (young
  22–25 workers in AI-exposed occupations losing ground), extending the ADP payroll data from Jul-2025
  through **June 2026.** The 22–25 AI-exposed employment gap widened to **~19%** (from ~16% in the Nov-2025
  version); absolute decline **~11%** for the two most-exposed quintiles vs **~+10%** for similarly-aged
  workers in less-exposed occupations (Nov-2022 → Jun-2026). Adjustment mechanism unchanged: **reduced
  hiring, not increased separations,** concentrated where AI *substitutes for* rather than *augments* tasks.
  **The genuinely new part:** the authors re-run the test against the confound this project flagged in W23 —
  the NY Fed finding that remote work, not AI, explains ~two-thirds of the same young-grad unemployment rise
  — and report the gap "remains when we exclude technology firms and computer occupations, when we control
  for exposure to interest-rate increases and **remote work**, … and when we use alternative measures of AI
  exposure." *Significance:* doesn't *settle* the AI-vs-remote-work dispute — no coefficient/effect size for
  the remote-work-controlled specification could be verified, only that the gap "remains" — but it is the
  paper's own authors directly engaging the exact unresolved confound, on updated data, with the divergence
  **widening rather than narrowing.** Strengthens the young-cohort anchor and narrows (without closing) the
  attribution dispute. → **folded into evidence_base** (Stanford/ADP entry extended with the Aug-2026 figures
  and the remote-work-control result, tagged 2026-W33). → follow-up: a dedicated primary-PDF read (once text
  extraction is possible) to check whether the remote-work-controlled coefficient is reported with a magnitude.
  https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/

## Running threads

- **UBI / cash-transfer wellbeing — the thread that moved, and moved substantively.** After weeks of "no new
  guaranteed-income primary result since the German RCT," the German RCT itself delivered the update: the
  wellbeing/purpose gains **persist 18 months after transfers end (91%)**, with relief fading and purpose
  holding. This is the sharpest UBI evidence the project has on the *durability* dimension, and it reframes
  the "Money buys relief, not meaning" slogan (relief is the *transient* part; the purpose gain is the
  *durable* part). **Track:** whether the individual-vs-household discretionary-control hinge replicates in
  other high-income pilots (the UK pilot, when it reports ~Nov 2026, is the nearest test). The parked
  candidates (MAGMA/Marienthal time-structure gains; Compton null) are unmoved.

## Watchlist (the analogy breakpoints)

- **What is being automated (muscle → routine cognition → judgment/creativity)?** No frontier-capability
  movement with a distinguishable labour/meaning angle this week (agentic-AI releases and EU AI Act August
  applicability are governance/adoption texture, filtered out per the manifesto). The Stanford/ADP update
  touches this indirectly: the harm remains concentrated where AI **substitutes for** rather than **augments**
  tasks — the substitution/augmentation line is where the displacement sits. **Movement:** none on the
  capability frontier; a confirming detail on *where* automation bites.
- **Speed vs society's adjustment timescale?** The German RCT is a *timescale* datum of a different kind: it
  shows a policy intervention's psychological benefit **outlasting** the intervention by 18+ months — i.e.
  meaning-relevant effects can be durable, not just switched on and off with the money. That's a (rare)
  positive on the adjustment-capacity side: if support can produce self-sustaining life changes, the
  adjustment window is more forgiving than a pure "income while it lasts" model implies. **Movement:** one
  durability datapoint, favourable, but N=107.
- **Does 'new jobs appear' still hold (or does AI do the new jobs too)?** No new instrument this week; the
  W30 BPC "Trapped Workers" mobility-network lens remains the newest tool on this variable. The Stanford/ADP
  update reinforces the reallocation reading (harm via *reduced hiring* into exposed occupations, growth in
  less-exposed ones) without adding a new mechanism. **Movement:** none of substance.
