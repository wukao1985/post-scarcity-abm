## The week in one paragraph

**The week the "Was it ever AI?" question got a credible answer on the *other* side — and the honest lesson is that the
answer tracks the ruler.** For months the base has accumulated national labour sources that lean *against* a clean AI
attribution: StatCan (W44, "a general cooling rather than an AI shock" — hiring fell *most* where AI reaches *least*), the
Bank of Korea (W35, "amplifying, not culprit"), the NY Fed postings null (no junior/senior divergence), the project's own
contract-buffer null. This week a **Federal Reserve** paper (Ahn & Carollo, posted Nov 17) read the other way: workers who
are high on **both** AI exposure **and** AI adoption have seen larger post-LLM declines in job-finding and job-switching,
their within-job activity churn is up, AI's earlier wage/hours lift has faded, and the dispersion of job-finding prospects
has widened "largely driven by AI factors," pushing the natural rate of unemployment up ~0.2pp (with the authors' own
"considerable uncertainty" attached). **This is not a reversal of the macro-null and must not be written as one** — the
mechanism is *reallocation* (within-firm task reorganisation and a reconfiguration of labour inputs), not a visible
aggregate collapse, and the November jobs print that could test the aggregate is not due until **Dec 4**. What it *is* is
the sharpest instance yet of the project's founding methodological point: **the sources that find AI and the sources that
don't are separated less by the data than by the measure.** Ahn & Carollo condition on *observed adoption* (Lightcast) —
the same "what is actually being deployed, not what could be" move that let Massenkoff & McCrory (Anthropic, W-base) see a
hiring-flow signal — while StatCan and the NY Fed use *exposure* constructs (what an occupation is in principle
automatable). On exposure, the youth weakness shows up hardest in low-exposure jobs (StatCan) → "not AI." On
adoption-conditioned flows, the most-exposed-and-adopting workers slow down → "AI reallocation." **Both can be honestly
true, and the disagreement is the finding:** the attribution question is *method-relative*, and "Was it ever AI?" should
from now on be asked as "measured how — by exposure, or by adoption?" Alongside it, the **OECD work-organisation paper**
adds a rare *job-quality* note on the meaning-of-work margin — AI-exposed occupations saw a *smaller* rise in monotonous
tasks (AI associated, correlationally, with *less* drudgery growth) — but it credits education more than AI and measures
task composition, not wellbeing, so it is texture for the augmentation axis, not a meaning result. Everything on the
*measured-meaning* core stayed quiet: no new on-axis UBI *wellbeing/purpose* result (German RCT durability, W33, remains
the frontier), no new AI-companion/relatedness study dated to the window (the Q3 cluster's state is W41's), no macro print
(November due Dec 4). The sweep's other hits were off-axis, below-bar, or prior-dated vendor/survey material
(ZipRecruiter, Challenger's AI-cited-layoffs tally, Korn Ferry, Robert Walters, an Imperial College Gen-Z usage survey),
plus the Nov-23 RAISE/Burning Glass entry-level report that falls just outside the window.

  labour source in this base to read *toward* an AI-specific effect, and it does so by conditioning on *adoption*

  *falsifiable* statement of the project's long-standing caution than the bare null. The within-firm task-reorganisation

## Watchlist (the analogy breakpoints)

- **What is being automated (muscle → routine cognition → judgment/creativity)?** No frontier-capability movement in
  window. Two oblique signals: Ahn & Carollo's finding that the most **exposed-and-adopting** cognitive workers are the
  ones whose job-finding slowed (consistent with "the near-term bite is deployed, routine-cognitive work"), and the OECD's
  association of AI exposure with **less monotony growth** (if real and AI-driven, AI absorbing the *monotonous* slice —
  the drudgery end, not the judgment end). **Movement:** none on raw capability; the composition signals both point at
  *routine* work as the margin AI is actually touching — one on the demand side (flows), one on the task-quality side.
- **Speed vs society's adjustment timescale?** No aggregate pace reading in window (next: Nov jobs, Dec 4). Ahn & Carollo's
  ~**+0.2pp natural-rate** estimate is the week's one quantified pace-of-friction signal — a *structural* (not cyclical)
  uptick attributed to AI reallocation, i.e. the adjustment is leaving a small permanent-ish residue — but it is uncertain
  and interpretive. The belief-side pace reading (ITDF: the public already feels the adjustment apparatus is failing) is
  unchanged. **Movement:** a small, uncertain *structural* friction estimate; felt pace unchanged.
- **Does 'new jobs appear' still hold (or does AI do the new jobs too)?** Ahn & Carollo speaks obliquely against the clean
  version: if the most-exposed-and-adopting workers face **lower job-finding** and **wider dispersion** in prospects, the
  new-roles absorption is at least *slower and more unequal* for exactly the workers AI touches — but the paper is about
  flows and reallocation, not a direct test of whether AI performs the new work. The Chandar/Stanford "senior expansion,
  not junior destruction" reallocation reading and the BPC escapability lens remain state of the art. **Movement:** a
  flow-side signal that absorption is slower/more dispersed for the AI-exposed-and-adopting; not a test of the mechanism.
