## The week in one paragraph

**A quiet in-niche week whose one visible motion was a *thread crossing into the mainstream*, not a new finding —
and whose real content was the sweep tidying up three prior-week items the routine had dropped.** The genuinely
W37-dated event of interest is that the **wage-compression reading reached headline framing**: CNBC ran it as "*AI
may not take your job but it may have pinched your paycheck already*" (Sep 13), leaning on the already-logged
Apollo/Slok wage number (AI-exposed real-wage growth ~6.7pp slower) and, more vividly, the **BLS Q2-2026 labor share
— 52.8%, the lowest in the series since 1947** (Productivity & Costs revised, Sep 3), with real hourly compensation
down 3.3% in the quarter. That is a real and striking macro datum, but it is **not new evidence for an AI claim**:
the labor-share decline is a decades-long structural trend and the real-comp drop is largely an inflation bite (the
same August CPI, +0.4% m/m, gasoline +3.9%, that landed Sep 11) — so the honest state of the wage-compression thread
is *more visible, no stronger*. Underneath that, the widened sweep recovered two BLS items the last two digests
missed, both of which strengthen the **macro-null** rather than complicate it. First, the **CES preliminary benchmark
revision (Aug 28): −79,000** for the year through March 2026 — a tenth of 2025's −911K and 2024's −818K, the smallest
markdown in years, which means the calm aggregate is **not** the overcounting artifact the prior two years' huge
revisions had made a live worry. Second, on the relatedness side, the **Elon University "Rise of AI Companions"
survey (Sep 2)** quantifies the *scale* premise under the project's Q3 cluster (27% of internet-using US adults have
"significant emotional or social interactions" with LLMs; ~40% of under-50s; 31% call the bot a friend) — prevalence
texture, not a wellbeing outcome. Everything else was the discipline of *not* logging: NBER/arXiv labour hits all
already-anchored, no new UBI primary result, no new Anthropic Index since June's "Cadences," and the loud AI-companion
coverage (CNBC Sep 10; the recirculating "My Boyfriend is AI" arXiv piece) reproduced already-anchored or
out-of-window material.

- **CES preliminary benchmark revision — −79,000** (BLS, released **Aug 28 2026**; prior-week, caught in the W37
  sweep). 🟢 (federal establishment-survey benchmark; preliminary, final due Feb 2027). *Shows:* total nonfarm
  employment for the 12 months through March 2026 revised **−79,000 (−0.1%)** (total private −178,000) — the
  **smallest benchmark markdown in years**, roughly a tenth of the **2025 preliminary −911K** (largest since ≥2000)
  and **2024 preliminary −818K** (final −589K). *Significance for the meaning niche:* a genuine **macro-null
  confirmation** on the aggregate spine. In 2024–25 the huge benchmark cuts raised a real question — was the calm
  aggregate partly a measurement artifact, with phantom job growth later erased? The 2026 answer is **no**: the
  establishment survey was **not** materially overstating employment this cycle, so the economic–meaning decoupling's
  starting premise (the aggregate really is calm) is firmer, not softer. *Limits:* preliminary (will be finalised
  Feb 2027); a benchmark is an accuracy correction, not a cause-attributed labour signal, and says nothing about AI.
  → **folded into evidence_base** (macro/labour texture note). https://www.bls.gov/news.release/prebmk.nr0.htm

- **BLS Productivity & Costs, Q2 2026 (revised) — labor share 52.8%, lowest since 1947** (released **Sep 3 2026**;
  prior-week, caught in the W37 sweep; foregrounded by CNBC Sep 13). 🟢 **as a datum / 🔴-contested as an AI claim.**
  *Shows:* the **labor share of nonfarm-business output fell to 52.8%, a post-1947 series low**; **real hourly
  compensation −3.3%** in the quarter (productivity +1.4%, unit labor costs +1.2%). *Significance for the meaning
  niche:* the *wage-side* texture under the **wage-compression watch-thread** (W35) — labour's slice of output at a
  record low while employment stays calm, which, *if* AI-driven, is the "price not headcount" meaning-threat (degrades
  the fair-exchange / extrinsic-reward dimension while leaving Jahoda's latent functions nominally intact).
  **Attribution caveat (load-bearing, and the honest point of logging it):** the labor-share decline is a
  **decades-long structural trend** (offshoring, capital deepening, superstar-firm concentration, weakened labour
  bargaining) and the Q2 real-comp drop is largely an **inflation bite** (August CPI +0.4% m/m, gasoline +3.9%) —
  **neither is AI-identified.** Held as 🟡/attribution-contested texture, **not** an AI-meaning anchor.
  → **folded into evidence_base** (macro/labour texture note). https://www.bls.gov/news.release/prod2.nr0.htm

- **Elon University, "The Rise of AI Companions" — prevalence survey** (Imagining the Digital Future Center + YouGov,
  N=4,268 US internet-using adults, fielded May 18–22 2026; **released Sep 2 2026**, WaPo coverage same day;
  prior-week, caught in the W37 sweep). 🟡 (single self-report survey; attitudes/prevalence, not a wellbeing outcome
  or causal design). *Shows:* **27%** of internet-using US adults report "significant emotional or social
  interactions" with LLMs (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot); **~40% of under-50s** qualify as "AI companion users"
  (>2× the over-50 rate); **31%** consider their chatbot a friend, another 15% "more complicated"; >⅓ say the bot
  agrees with them too much; 15% say it makes them feel less in touch with reality. *Significance for the meaning
  niche:* it does not test *whether* companions harm well-being (Zhang et al., W34, is the causal-conditional anchor)
  — it sizes the **exposed population**, i.e. the *scale* premise under the whole Q3 cluster. Zhang's conditional
  harm (concentrated in the already-isolated companionship-seeker) matters more if the companionship-seeking base is
  large; this survey says it is, and skews young. *Not anchored:* self-report attitudes, single survey — noted as
  Q3 *scale texture*, not a new 🟢 leg. (Naming collision to avoid: this Elon report shares its title with Zhang et
  al.'s arXiv preprint 2506.12605; they are different works — Elon = prevalence survey, Zhang = coded-transcript
  wellbeing study.) https://www.elon.edu/u/news/2026/09/02/imagining-the-digital-future-center-reports-on-the-rise-of-ai-companions/
