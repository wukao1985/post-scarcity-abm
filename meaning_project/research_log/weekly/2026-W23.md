## The week in one paragraph

A substantive week that pulled in two directions at once. On the **meaning layer** it was the
strongest run yet: a convergent cluster on **relatedness and its erosion** — OECD (young men are
simultaneously the loneliest cohort *and* the most AI-exposed), Aalto/CHI (AI companions
*crowd out* human connection rather than substitute for it), and Bousquet (the *desocialization*
of gig/low-wage work is already here) — landing on top of last week's Emile mortality finding to
make Q3 ("can AI supply mattering?") lean harder toward **no**. A parallel cluster sharpened the
*how-you-use-it* thread: Ranjit/CHI shows the tasks workers find **agentic and happy are
disproportionately the ones being automated**; Passi names the lag ("asymptomatic harm,"
"intuition rust"); Giuntella reconciles the aggregate null with Lee et al.'s mechanism; BCG finds
freeing time ≠ meaningful time (66% get no framework for the hours AI returns). But the week's
real jolt cuts the *other* way and is the honest headline: a **NY Fed** post (Emanuel, Harrington
& Pallais) finds **remote work — not AI — explains ~two-thirds of the rise in young-graduate
unemployment in 2022–24**, with AI exposure *not* explaining the divergence in that window. That
is a direct confounder for the project's clearest 🟢 labour signal (Stanford/ADP young-cohort
drop) and the empirical basis for "experience creep." The week's throughline: the **meaning**
evidence is consolidating fast, while the **economic** "AI is already hitting the young" claim
just got materially weaker. We hold both honestly — and downgrade confidence on the second.

## Notable items

- **"Remote work — not AI — has sidelined recent college graduates"** — Emanuel (NY Fed),
  Harrington (UVA) & Pallais (Harvard); NY Fed *Liberty Street Economics*, 1 Jun 2026. 🟢
  (federal employment data + a remotability index, plus a single Fortune-500 tech-firm case
  study; observational, not causal-clean). **The week's most important item, and a confounder
  for our own evidence base.** *What it shows:* unemployment among college grads under 29 rose
  ~20% post-pandemic (2022–24 vs 2017–19) while older grads' held/fell; the gap is concentrated
  in **"remotable" jobs**; remote work accounts for **~64% (nearly two-thirds)** of the rise.
  Mechanism: firms hiring remotely shy away from juniors who need mentoring — at the case firm,
  engineers got ~20% more feedback when sitting near colleagues, and the firm shifted hiring
  ~a decade older after going remote, then reversed on return-to-office. Crucially: **AI
  exposure "didn't explain the divergence" in 2022–24** (authors note it *could* in later years).
  *Significance:* the Stanford/ADP "Canaries" young-cohort decline — our single clearest 🟢 of
  AI labour harm — now has a well-identified rival explanation for the *same period*. This does
  **not** refute the AI reading (ADP found the drop concentrated where AI *automates*; the two
  use different identification), but it means the young-cohort dip can no longer be confidently
  pinned on AI, and "experience creep" loses some of its AI attribution. A genuine honesty
  update, in the same spirit as our contract-buffer null and the "SO was a special case" reading.
  https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/

- **"Are We Automating the Joy Out of Work?"** — Ranjit, Zhou, Swayamditta & Quercia;
  arXiv:2603.14963; CHI 2026. 🟢 (survey of workers/developers rating 171 tasks, LM-scaled to
  10,131 O*NET tasks) / 🔴 (design prescriptions). *What it shows:* tasks workers associate with
  **agency and happiness are disproportionately AI-exposed** vs tasks they rate routine/low-
  meaning; plus a worker↔developer mismatch on what AI should optimise. *Limits:* self-reported
  meaning ratings, LM-inferred scaling, design-advocacy venue, no causal/longitudinal arm.
  *Significance:* reframes the risk from "some jobs vanish" to "**the parts of jobs that produce
  wellbeing are the parts being targeted**." Supplies the *task-selection* layer above Lee et
  al.'s individual *mechanism*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14963

- **"Mental Health Impacts of AI Companions"** — Aalto University; CHI 2026. 🟢 (quasi-experiment;
  ~2,000 Replika users' Reddit language, year-before vs year-after, matched controls; single
  platform, observational). *What it shows:* short-term comfort, but over time **more** loneliness,
  depression and suicidal-ideation signals than controls; mechanism is a **crowd-out** — AI
  companionship "raises the perceived cost of human relationships," so users reach out less.
  *Significance:* the behavioural counterpart to the "people prefer human empathy" preference
  result already in evidence_base; together they push Q3 toward **AI cannot supply relatedness,
  and deployed as a loneliness fix it may backfire.**
  https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790558

- **OECD, "Social Connections and Loneliness in OECD Countries"** — OECD, Oct 2025. 🟢 (official
  cross-national survey; descriptive, not causal). *What it shows:* in-person interaction
  declining across the OECD; **young people (16–24) and men** saw the steepest recent
  deteriorations; loneliness + low interaction associated with ~**871,000 premature deaths/yr**
  globally. *Significance:* demographic precision the canon (Jahoda, Case & Deaton) lacks — the
  socially most-at-risk group (young men) is the *same* group taking the early entry-level hit.
  Names a concrete "double-exposure" population. Pairs with Emile's mortality mediation.
  https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/social-connections-and-loneliness-in-oecd-countries_6df2d6a0-en.html

- **"AI and the wellbeing of workers"** — Giuntella, Koenig & Stella; *Scientific Reports*
  15:20087 (Jun 2025). 🟢 (SOEP, two decades, DiD/event-study; observational; data ends ~2020).
  *What it shows:* **no average harm** from AI occupational exposure to mental health/wellbeing
  (slight health gains via reduced physical intensity) — **but** restricting to workers who
  *actually report using AI tools* shows "indications of declining life and job satisfaction."
  *Significance:* the reconciliation anchor — the population-average null (Giuntella) and the
  task-level mechanism (Lee et al. — heavy passive users harmed) operate at different levels;
  the actual-user nuance bridges them and warns the null may not survive deeper AI penetration.
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-98241-3

- **Passi et al., "From Future of Work to Future of Workers" (asymptomatic AI harms)** —
  Microsoft Research; arXiv:2601.21920; CHI 2026. 🟡 (year-long qualitative study of oncologists;
  single domain). *What it shows:* short-term operational gains alongside slow **"intuition
  rust"** and "identity commoditization," invisible in productivity metrics — "asymptomatic
  harm." *Significance:* extends Lee et al. into a longitudinal, high-stakes setting and supplies
  vocabulary for *why the harm is under-reported*: benefit is immediate, cost accrues silently.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21920

- **Bousquet, "Lonely Work: Gig Work, Workplace Technology and Working-Class Loneliness"** —
  *Journal of Business Ethics*, 16 Mar 2026. 🔴 (normative) / 🟡 (empirically-informed; no
  original data). *What it shows:* gig platforms + automation + efficiency-monitoring have
  already **desocialized** low-wage work — stripping Jahoda's latent functions — and it lands
  hardest on workers who can't exit. *Significance:* the meaning crisis has a *present-tense*
  version for low-wage workers, not just a future post-labour one; sharpens the project's class
  dimension. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-026-06289-6

- **Macro/labour texture (logged this week, 🟢 with caveats):** **Goldman Sachs** payroll
  decomposition (gross AI substitution ~25K jobs/mo, augmentation ~+9K, net ~−11–16K; the
  "new jobs" are data-centre *construction*, not re-employed admin workers — proprietary, via
  journalism); **BLS May 2026** (+172K payrolls, U 4.3%, but financial activities −107K/12mo and
  information −342K since Nov-22 persist in high-exposure sectors — BLS assigns no cause);
  **Dallas Fed TBOS** (66% of TX firms use AI; 10% already cut headcount need, 30% expect to);
  **NACEWEB** (entry-level AI-skill demand ~tripled in two survey quarters; **40% of early-career
  workers report changing career plans because of AI**). Together: macro still calm, sector
  pattern cohering, skills goalposts moving faster than any retraining cycle.

- **(June 8 scan) BCG, "AI Is Reshaping Jobs Faster Than Companies Are Reshaping Work"** — BCG,
  3 Jun 2026 (N=11,749, 14 markets). 🟡 (proprietary consulting survey). *What it shows:* 42% of
  regular AI users save ≥1 workday/week, but **66% get no guidance on what to do with the freed
  time**; 47% now spend more time *managing* AI than doing the work; a "joy paradox" (satisfaction
  up *and* cognitive load up). *Significance:* the empirical echo of the project's core worry —
  **freed time is not automatically meaningful time**; the default is drift.
  https://www.bcg.com/press/3june2026-ai-reshaping-jobs-faster-than-companies-reshaping-work

## Running threads

- **The entry rung — now genuinely contested.** Last week "experience creep" had a clean
  AI story (Stanford/ADP + NY Fed postings + Washington Monthly). This week the NY Fed
  (Emanuel/Harrington/Pallais) attributes ~two-thirds of the young-grad unemployment rise to
  **remote work, not AI**, in 2022–24. The drop is real; its *cause* is now disputed between
  AI-automation and remote-work-driven mentorship loss. **Track:** later-year updates (the
  authors flag AI could matter more post-2024) and any reconciliation with the ADP "concentrated
  where AI automates" finding.
- **Relatedness as the AI-resistant core — consolidating toward "no."** Empathy-preference
  (prior) + Aalto crowd-out (behavioural) + OECD demographics + Bousquet desocialization +
  Emile mortality. The evidence that AI *cannot* supply mattering, and may erode it, is now the
  project's best-supported meaning claim. **Track:** any RCT (not quasi-experiment) on AI
  companions; replication of the crowd-out beyond Replika.
- **Augmentation/automation as psychology — still gaining.** Ranjit (meaning-rich tasks targeted)
  + Passi (intuition rust, delayed harm) + Giuntella (actual-users worse) thicken the Lee et al.
  spine. **Track:** independent replication of the agency/happiness-exposure mapping.
- **"New jobs" mechanism — still not closing the loop.** Goldman + BLS agree the jobs being
  created (construction, hospitality, health, local gov) are not in AI-exposed roles and don't
  re-employ displaced admin/finance/info workers. The reassurance keeps narrowing.

## Watchlist (the analogy breakpoints)

- **What is being automated (muscle → routine cognition → judgment/creativity)?** Still routine
  cognition — but Ranjit adds a disquieting wrinkle: *within* exposed work, the **agency- and
  happiness-producing micro-tasks** are disproportionately targeted. No clean climb into
  judgment/creativity yet: the month's frontier releases (Gemini 3.5 tier, agent stacks) and
  agent benchmarks (**ClawBench best ~33%; KellyBench — every frontier model finished in the
  red**) show autonomous real-world economic performance is still weak. **Movement:** capability
  ceiling rising; reliable *autonomy* not yet here.
- **Speed vs society's adjustment timescale?** NACEWEB's skills demand ~tripling in two survey
  quarters is the fastest churn logged — quarterly goalposts vs 1–4-year retraining cycles. Passi
  adds a *psychological* lag: harm is asymptomatic, recognised only once chronic. **Movement:**
  adjustment-speed breakpoint tightening on both the skills and the harm-recognition sides.
- **Does 'new jobs appear' still hold (or does AI do the new jobs too)?** Goldman: net new jobs
  are physical-infrastructure construction, not re-employment of the displaced; BLS confirms the
  growth is in low-AI-exposure sectors. **Movement:** mechanism still runs, but visibly off-target
  for the exposed cohort.
