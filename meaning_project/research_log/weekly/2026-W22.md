## The week in one paragraph

A substantive week, and unusually it moved on **both** the project's layers at once. On the
**economic** layer the picture sharpened around a single site — the **entry rung of the career
ladder** — and around a mechanism with a name: **"experience creep"** (junior roles quietly
mutating into mid-level ones as routine work is absorbed by AI), rather than visible mass
layoffs. On the **psychological** layer two strong 🟢 anchors landed: passive AI use erodes
self-efficacy, ownership and felt meaning while *active* collaboration preserves them
(Lee et al.), and **purpose erosion is a measurable mediator of mortality** (Emile). The
week's genuine throughline is that these two layers rhyme on the same axis: the
**augmentation-vs-automation** distinction is turning out to be simultaneously the *economic*
breakpoint and the *psychological* one — how a person uses AI may decide both whether their job
survives and whether their sense of meaning does. The week also produced a small but
instructive **honesty save**: a daily-scan flag (Yale Budget Lab implying Claude usage had
"flipped" to automation-dominant) did **not** survive contact with the primary Anthropic
March-2026 report — augmentation still leads on Claude.ai; only the API automated further.

## Notable items

- **"How AI Broke the Entry-Level Job"** — *Washington Monthly*, 29 May 2026. 🟡 (journalism
  wrapping mixed 🟢/survey data). Names the mechanism: **"experience creep"** — entry-level
  roles "becoming more like mid-level roles" as routine tasks are stripped out. Cites ~3M
  white-collar jobs added since ChatGPT (software devs +7%, radiologists +10%, paralegals +21%),
  professional-services real wages +5%, **but** long-term unemployment among bachelor's holders
  up from ~1-in-5 to ~1-in-3, and tech postings open to 2–4-yr experience falling 46%→40% (mid-22
  to mid-25) while 5+-yr postings rose 37%→42%. A Strada Institute survey of ~1,500 executives is
  *more sanguine* (46% say AI **increased** entry-level hiring vs 13% decreased) — a real tension
  to hold, and a reminder the survey is executive self-report. *Significance:* gives the
  young-cohort signal (Stanford ADP, NY Fed) a named, trackable mechanism — the rung isn't
  removed, it's raised out of reach.
  https://washingtonmonthly.com/2026/05/29/ai-entry-level-jobs-college-graduates/

- **Anthropic Economic Index — "Learning curves" report** — Anthropic, Mar 2026 (Nov 2025→Feb
  2026 data). 🟢 (single-provider usage). *Resolves the 2026-06-01 watchlist flag.* The primary
  report states **augmentation increased slightly on Claude.ai** (driven by validation/learning
  patterns) while **automation decreased sharply in the 1P API** — opposite trajectories per
  surface. So the Yale Budget Lab secondary framing ("Claude usage now more likely automation
  than augmentation") conflates API with consumer; on the **consumer** surface augmentation still
  leads (>half). New finding: a **10% higher conversation success rate for 6-month+ tenured
  users** (after controls) — "skill-biased technological change," early-adopter advantages
  compounding. *Significance:* the project's "augmentation-dominant on the human-facing side"
  characterisation **holds** — and we kept it honestly, by checking primary rather than the
  headline. https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report

- **Lee et al., "Relying on AI at work reduces self-efficacy, ownership, and meaning while active
  collaboration mitigates the effects"** — *Scientific Reports* 16:13583 (2026). 🟢
  (pre-registered, N=269 + N=270). **Passive** AI use (copy the output) reliably undermined
  self-efficacy, psychological ownership and perceived meaningfulness — and the declines
  **persisted** after returning to manual work; **active** collaboration (draft first, then
  AI-refine) preserved all three at levels indistinguishable from independent work.
  *Significance:* AI use *per se* doesn't corrode the psychological sources of meaning — *how*
  you use it does. The augmentation/automation split is a **psychological** category, not only an
  economic one. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-42312-6

- **Emile, "Loneliness predicts mortality risk via the erosion of purpose in life"** — *Social
  Science & Medicine* 368, 23 Jan 2026. 🟢 (prospective; HRS, N=8,351, 11-yr follow-up). Purpose
  in life statistically explains **~88%** of the loneliness→premature-mortality association;
  mediation runs through *changes* in purpose over time; robust to depression, isolation,
  neuroticism. *Limits:* observational, older sample (mean ~68) — causality/working-age
  generalisation need care. *Significance:* the clearest 🟢 yet that "purpose erosion" is not a
  soft cultural worry but a **measurable, mortal** health variable — directly grounds Q3
  ("can AI supply mattering?") and the "meaning as a health variable" framing.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0277953626000833

- **IMF Staff Discussion Note SDN/2026/001, "Bridging Skill Gaps … New Jobs Creation in the AI
  Age"** — Goraya, Mendes Tavares et al., IMF, 9 Jan 2026. 🟢 (government report; job-postings).
  New-skill postings carry a 2–3.4% wage premium and +1.3pp local employment — **but
  AI-specific** skill postings carry higher wages (~7.5–8% UK) with **no overall employment
  gain** in local US markets; the middle gets no significant benefit; polarisation reinforced.
  *Significance:* completes the watchlist item flagged 05-31. The "new jobs" mechanism survives
  for general new skills, but for *AI-specific* skills it shows up as **wages without
  headcount** — winners at the top, no lift for the middle.
  https://www.imf.org/en/publications/staff-discussion-notes/issues/2026/01/09/bridging-skill-gaps-for-the-future-new-jobs-creation-in-the-ai-age-572136

- **(Noted, low weight)** Jabra × Happiness Research Institute claim that daily AI users report
  higher job/life satisfaction. 🔴/PR — industry-sponsored, cross-sectional, self-report,
  selection-confounded (people who adopt AI may already be flourishing). *Not* an anchor; logged
  only so a future scan doesn't mistake it for evidence. Contrast with Lee et al.'s controlled
  finding that *passive* use erodes meaning. https://workplaceinsight.net/new-study-suggests-ai-could-be-the-key-to-workplace-wellbeing/

## Running threads

- **The entry rung as the first visible site.** Stanford ADP (~16% young-cohort drop), NY Fed
  postings, MIT Tech Review, and now Washington Monthly's "experience creep" converge: AI's
  earliest labour effect is concentrated at the **bottom of the career ladder**, via a raised
  threshold rather than mass dismissal. Track whether the rung is rebuilt (new junior tasks
  appear) or stays hollowed.
- **Augmentation vs automation as a dual axis.** Now tracked on *two* layers: economic (which
  usage type destroys jobs) and psychological (which usage type destroys meaning — Lee et al.).
  Anthropic Mar-2026: augmentation still leads on Claude.ai, automation rising on API. Watch
  whether the consumer surface follows the API.
- **"New jobs" mechanism, qualified.** IMF: holds for general new skills; for AI-specific skills
  it delivers *wages without employment* and skips the middle. The reassurance is narrowing.
- **Purpose as a health variable.** Emile adds a mortality endpoint to the meaning literature.
  Track replication in working-age samples — that would close the biggest generalisation gap.

## Watchlist (the analogy breakpoints)

- **What is being automated (muscle → routine cognition → judgment/creativity)?** Still routine
  cognition. Anthropic Mar-2026: coding migrating from augmentative (Claude.ai) toward automated
  (API); customer-service and automated-trading API workflows doubled Nov-25→Feb-26. No clean
  move into judgment/creativity yet. **Movement:** automation deepening *within* already-exposed
  routine-cognitive tasks, not (yet) climbing to judgment.
- **Speed vs society's adjustment timescale?** "Experience creep" is a slow, quiet adjustment —
  the labour market is absorbing AI through *hiring thresholds*, not headcount shocks, which may
  mask the speed of underlying capability change ("Crashing Waves," 05-31). **Movement:**
  adjustment is happening at the margin of entry, where it's hardest to see and easiest to
  under-react to.
- **Does 'new jobs appear' still hold (or does AI do the new jobs too)?** IMF gives the sharpest
  read this cycle: new AI-skill demand exists but converts to *wages, not jobs*, and bypasses the
  middle. **Movement:** the mechanism is visibly weakening for the AI-specific slice.
