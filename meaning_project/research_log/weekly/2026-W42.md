Chinese users) that folds under the De Freitas reconciliation already logged in W41, not new legs. What the widened
sweep *did* surface was a **genuine, previously-unlogged catch** on the best-labour-evidenced thread: the **US Census
Bureau young-worker papers**. **Lee Tucker's "You're (not) Hired"** (CES-WP-26-27, Apr 2026) uses the **Quarterly
Workforce Indicators** (matched employer-employee administrative data, near-universal US private-sector coverage) and
finds regression-adjusted employment of **22–24-year-olds in the most AI-exposed quintile of industry-state cells fell
~12% over the 10 quarters after ChatGPT**, with less-exposed industries stable; **Orr, Tucker & Warren's "Graduating
into Disruption"** (CES-WP-26-56, Sep 2026) uses **LEHD college-graduate records** and finds graduates in the **most
AI-exposed decile of college majors** saw **initial-employment likelihood fall 5 percentage points and full-quarter
initial earnings fall 13%** — a hit the authors call **"comparable in magnitude to the earnings losses associated with
graduating into a large recession,"** roughly half of it from exposed graduates **shifting into lower-wage sectors such
as restaurants and retail.** This matters for three reasons: (i) it is a **third independent US data source** for the
young-cohort signal — Census administrative micro-data, neither ADP (Stanford) nor LinkedIn-visible multinationals
(Chandar) — so the signal is no longer vulnerable to a single-sample worry; (ii) it supplies a **new identification
margin** (field of study at the clearly-defined point of labour-market entry) and a **vivid meaning-relevant** outcome
(exposed graduates landing in restaurants/retail, not the white-collar jobs they trained for); and (iii) it **reopens,
honestly, the W39 question** — Chandar's international firm-level design found junior employment only **−2.5%
(n.s.)**, driving the "senior expansion, not junior destruction" nuance, whereas these US administrative papers find
**absolute, significant** young-worker declines (−12% QWI; −5pp / −13% LEHD-by-major). The honest reconciliation is a
**unit-and-geography** one (Chandar reads *within-firm composition across 41 countries*; Tucker/Orr read *US
cohort-level entry*), and it means the project should stop presenting "senior expansion, not junior destruction" as the
settled shape — in US entry-level micro-data the hit is real and large. The attribution caveat survives intact: Tucker
himself is hedged (young-worker slippage visible from the COVID onset; remote work and rising educational attainment
flagged as possible explanations; monetary policy explains **at most ~a quarter**; he even suggests firms "paused
junior hiring out of *uncertainty about* AI rather than because they were *using* it"). Alongside the catch, the sweep
turned up a would-be headline — "**October jobs report canceled**" — that a date check dissolved: the article is **Nov
19 2025**, about the **2025** shutdown and the **October 2025** reference period (whose household-survey data were never
collected), *not* anything new in 2026; the September 2026 report printed on time (Oct 2, +29K), the October 2026 report

  **(Tucker/QWI)** early-career **hires fell immediately and sharply after ChatGPT** in the industry-state cells most

  quintile fell ~12% over the 10 quarters** after ChatGPT's release, with less-exposed industries stable (the hiring
  *rate* "largely recovered by early 2025," which the author attributes to a *smaller employment base*, not a
  rebound). **(Orr/Tucker/Warren/LEHD)** post-graduation **employment, earnings and job-switching among the most
  AI-exposed college majors began to diverge immediately after ChatGPT in late 2022**; the most AI-exposed **decile of
  majors** saw **initial-employment likelihood decline 5 percentage points** and **full-quarter initial earnings decline
  13%** — the authors state this earnings loss is **"comparable in magnitude to the earnings losses associated with
  graduating into a large recession"** — with **roughly half** of the earnings decline from a **shift into lower-wage
  sectors such as restaurants and retail** and the remainder from lower earnings *within* sectors; effects **attenuate
  with time from entry but remain substantial** for the most-exposed majors. *Significance for the meaning niche:* this
  is the **third independent US data source** confirming the young-cohort signal (after Stanford/ADP and the
  international Chandar firm-level study), and the first built on **near-universal Census administrative micro-data** —
  so the project's "edges fray first / experience creep" thread is no longer exposed to the single-sample worry that
  dogged ADP (one payroll processor) or Chandar (LinkedIn multinationals). The **Graduating-into-Disruption** outcome is
  the most *meaning-legible* labour datum in the base: graduates who made costly human-capital investments in AI-exposed
  fields entering the labour market into **restaurants and retail**, with a recession-scale earnings hit, is the
  broken-developmental-arc story the "raised rung" concept predicts, now measured at the clean point of entry. **The
  load-bearing complication (and the honest reason this is not just a confirmation):** these US administrative papers
  find an **absolute, significant** young-worker decline (−12% QWI; −5pp / −13% LEHD), whereas Chandar & Klein

  honest update is that **the W39 "relative-not-absolute" headline is a firm-level, international reading; at the US
  entry-cohort level the effect is absolute and large.** *Limits (state honestly, and load-bearing):* working papers,
  not peer-reviewed; exposure (not deployment) constructs; and the **attribution stays contested** — Tucker locates
