# Concepts — the project's own coinages & frameworks

As the writing develops, the goal is a few signature concepts others can cite — the move from
"person who writes essays" to "person who proposed a framework." Seed them here; refine as
they recur across articles.

Status: 🌰 seed · 🌿 used in ≥1 article · 🌳 signature (recurs, others cite)

## 🌰 The three-layer honesty framework (🟢/🟡/🔴)
Empirical / analogical / normative-design claims, never blurred. The project's methodological
spine. → likely the first article.

## 🌰 The economic–meaning decoupling
Even in the most economically optimistic post-labour future (jobs appear, UBI is ample), the
*meaning* question stays fully open. Separating these two debates is itself a contribution,
because ~99% of discourse conflates them.

## 🌰 "Money buys relief, not meaning"
The recurring 🟢 finding from cash-transfer/lottery/retirement evidence: money reliably removes
material stress but is a weak lever on purpose/meaning.

## 🌰 The retreat that may run out of high ground
Each automation wave pushed humans to higher cognitive ground; AI targets that ground itself.
The open question of whether a "next rung" exists. (Analogy with an explicit breakpoint.)

## 🌰 The "new-jobs mechanism" precondition
The historical reassurance ("new jobs always appear") secretly assumes humans are better than
machines at the new jobs. Name and interrogate that precondition.

## 🌰 The augmentation/automation axis as a *dual* breakpoint
The augmentation-vs-automation distinction is doing double duty. *Economically* it predicts where
jobs go (automation-type usage → the entry-rung employment signal; augmentation-type → growth, e.g.
software devs). *Psychologically* it predicts where meaning goes (Lee et al. 2026: passive/automation
use erodes self-efficacy/ownership/meaning and persists; active/augmentation use preserves them).
The same axis governs both layers — which makes "keep humans in the augmentative loop" a candidate
🔴 design principle, not just an economic forecast. (Strengthened 2026-W22.)

## 🌰 Experience creep / the raised rung
AI's first labour effect arrives not as mass dismissal but as a *raised threshold*: routine junior
tasks are absorbed, so "entry-level" roles quietly demand mid-level experience and the bottom rung
of the career ladder disappears. Names the mechanism behind the young-cohort employment signal — and
locates where post-labour meaning-loss would surface first (those who can't get on the ladder at
all). (Seeded 2026-W22, from Washington Monthly + Stanford/ADP + NY Fed.)
**⚠️ Caveat added 2026-W23:** the *AI attribution* of this mechanism is now contested. The NY Fed
(Emanuel/Harrington/Pallais, Jun 2026) finds **remote work** explains ~two-thirds of the young-grad
unemployment rise in 2022–24, with AI exposure not explaining the divergence in that window. The
raised-rung phenomenon is real; whether AI (vs remote-work mentorship loss) is the cause is open.
Keep the concept, drop the certainty.

## 🌰 Meaning-targeted automation
Automation is not meaning-neutral within a job: the micro-tasks workers find most agentic and
satisfying are disproportionately the ones being offloaded to AI (Ranjit et al., CHI 2026). So even
where augmentation nominally "leads," the *good parts* can be the parts that go first. Reframes the
risk from job loss to meaning loss, and points to task-allocation design as a 🔴 lever. (Seeded
2026-W23.)

## 🌰 Relatedness crowd-out (AI companionship is not a substitute)
AI companions don't fill the relatedness gap — they *raise the perceived cost* of effortful human
connection, so users reach out to people less and the deficit deepens over time (Aalto/CHI 2026),
consistent with the empathy-preference result (care's value depends partly on a *human* source).
Design implication: companions as *bridges* to human connection, not endpoints. (Seeded 2026-W23.)

## Borrowed terms worth tracking (not our coinages)
- **"Asymptomatic harm" / "intuition rust"** (Passi et al., Microsoft/CHI 2026): AI's benefits are
  immediate and measured; its costs to skill/identity accrue silently and are recognised only once
  chronic — which is *why* they're under-reported and under-regulated.
- **"Desocialization"** (Bousquet, J. Business Ethics 2026): the structural stripping of Jahoda's
  latent social functions from gig/automated/monitored low-wage work — the present-tense version of
  the post-work meaning problem.

## 🌰 Meaning infrastructure / manufacturing "mattering"
If contribution-to-others is what UBI and virtual worlds can't supply, what would institutions
that deliberately produce "mattering" look like? (Design / 🔴.)
